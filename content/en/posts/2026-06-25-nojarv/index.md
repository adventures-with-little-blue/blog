# What Happens After Evening Orienteering in Norrnäs?

Time for another chapter in the series “on the way home from evening orienteering”. This time I visited Närpes OK's Thursday event at Kompassberget in Norrnäs, Närpes. On the way home there was then more orienteering when the navigation app had different ideas about where we were going than I did. I made it to Lake Nojärv in any case, after much trouble and confusion.

<!--more-->

![Norrskogen Wind Farm](norrskogen.jpg "Norrskogen wind farm.")

The organisers of the Thursday event took registrations at the foot of a roughly 200 m high wind turbine, since the location is in the Norrskogen wind farm. The turbines are so mighty that it is hard to really capture them in a photo. The orienteering did not go particularly brilliantly. One really major mistake of more than 20 minutes. My plan was to head for a path and then turn off towards the control, but the path, which according to the map should have been obvious, was in practice invisible. I walked past the path and spent the next 15 minutes swearing my way through sometimes very dense forest and sometimes the cleared trunks of very dense forest. Then I made a so-called parallel error, when I thought I had found the path I was heading for, but it turned out to be a logging track that was not on the map. To make matters worse, I made another small mistake when trying to follow the same path in another direction, and lost the path again. I found all the controls in the end at least, so it became an honourable last place in the results list.

![Wind Turbine](vindmolla.jpg "Registration and finish at the base of the wind turbine. There were not many people left there when I finally reached the finish.")

After spending 1.5 hours in the forest, I headed north towards Rangsby. I took a very quick look at the Rangsby village church. It is a little different. It was built by volunteers in the early 1900s, partly with materials from four windmills. Today it is maintained by an association, not by the Närpes parish.

![Rangsby Village Church](rangsby_bykyrka.jpg "Rangsby village church.")

I continued via Träskböle towards Taklax, mostly along narrow gravel roads. In Taklax I visited the harbour in Kamb by Hinjärv träsk. I ate a banana, watched the pied flycatchers and enjoyed the peace for a while.

![Kamb](kamb.jpg "The boat harbour in Kamb, Taklax. In the background you can glimpse the turbines of the Norrskogen wind farm.")

After Kamb the problems began. My navigator, Kurviger, wanted me to follow a forest road north towards Bjurbäck, but I decided to ride through Taklax village instead. Since I had not set any waypoints along the forest road, I thought the navigator would understand that I had chosen another route and adapt. But no. In Bjurbäck the navigator told me to turn off in the wrong direction. It took a while before I realised I was on the wrong road. And I could not get the damned navigator to give in either, I had to type Korsbäck into Google Maps instead.

In Korsbäck I started Kurviger again, because I had the planned route there. It guided me perfectly correctly onto the road towards Viitala village, but when the road split it did not tell me to turn left, nor did it point out that I had gone wrong. After quite a while I was suddenly back at the road between Bjurbäck and Korsbäck again, so I had ridden in a circle.

![Wild Viitala](vilda_viitala.jpg "On the way towards Viitala village: the sign says Wild Viitala.")

Instead I rode back along the forest road, and this time I managed to turn the right way towards Viitala. In Viitala a hiking trail begins towards Velkmossen, and there is also a bird tower with a view over Lake Nojärv. I walked the not quite one kilometre to the bird tower in the company of a million mosquitoes. Up in the tower, thankfully, there was enough wind to keep most of them away. I drank my coffee from a thermos I had brought along. A few cranes walked about out on the mire, otherwise not much happened.

![The Viitala Bird Tower](fageltorn.jpg "The Viitala bird tower by Lake Nojärv.")

![Nojärv träsk](nojarv.jpg "Lake Nojärv")

![Nojärv träsk](nojarv2.jpg "Lake Nojärv. Here too the turbines in Norrskogen were visible, but they do not show in the photo.")

When I got back to the motorcycle, I realised that it was getting late and that I would probably have to skip most of the other escapades I had planned. I rode out to Petalax, then via Mamrelund out to Strandvägen and then straight home. No more forest roads that day. In total it became 176 km of riding, almost half of it on gravel. Somewhat shamefully, this modest route is actually the second longest I have done so far this year. Improvement is required.


### About Navigation Apps

I have actually been quite happy with [Kurviger](https://kurviger.de), but now my patience is starting to wear thin. The business of deviating from the route has always been troublesome. I would like the app to update the route to guide me to the next waypoint, but that does not always work correctly. It seems to have its own internal waypoints that get in the way. Skipping a waypoint has also caused problems. It is supposed to be possible via Android Auto, but I have not managed to get it to work, so in practice I have most often stopped the motorcycle, stopped the navigation on my phone, and then started the navigation again. Then it usually understands that it should skip the waypoint I have already passed.

I have liked that Kurviger lets you plan routes in advance on the computer. But lately the web interface has been very slow. Saved routes do not load, you have to try several times before it agrees to load them. Sometimes the map disappears, in which case you have to reload the whole app. And sometimes it stops calculating the route and gives some vague error message. It feels as if this is turning into “abandonware”, meaning that the developers are no longer putting resources into the app.

Unfortunately I have not found any good alternative. Calimoto only knows paved roads, which does not work especially well in Finland. Google has a limit of 12 waypoints, whereas I usually have around 50 on my longer routes. I have not found any other alternatives with planning on a computer and decent Android Auto navigation and support for both asphalt and gravel. There has been some advertising for Stegra.io lately, but partly I have not managed to find out what it can and cannot do, and partly it seems to be very focused on gravel and forest roads.
