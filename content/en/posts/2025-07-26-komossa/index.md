## Try a Beach: Komossa Beach

The wildly popular series "Try a Beach" continues (as long as the daytime temperature exceeds +25°C). We head out with the bike and take a swim. This time we’re testing _Komossa Beach_.

Komossa, KAJ member [Kevin's](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Holmstr%C3%B6m) hometown, is a bit off the beaten path, but you get there along great motorcycle roads. Now we’ll find out if it’s also worth taking a dip when you’re out cruising.

<!--more-->

### How to get there

If you start from the Vaasa direction, you can conveniently begin by riding upstream along the north bank of the Kyrö river all the way to *Storkyro* church village. There, turn up along Ruusupurontie and continue past the new church. Ride until the road ends in Kaurajärvi, Vörå. But be careful—just after the municipal border to Vörå, there are a couple of sudden, sharp curves that have surely caused a few motorcyclists to go off the road over the years. The pavement isn’t great but mostly acceptable.

In Kaurajärvi, turn left towards Vörå, ride a hundred meters on the main road, then turn right towards *Oravais* along Keskisvägen. Follow this road to Keskis prayer house, where you turn right towards Komossa. Now just ride straight through Komossa city, but note the turnoff towards Alahärmä in the middle of the village. About 1.4 km after the turnoff to Alahärmä, a small gravel road goes off to the left, signposted "Fritisområde". Follow it and you’re there. Take it easy—there’s some loose sand on the road.

When you’re done swimming, continue towards Oravais. Feel free to stop and check out Kimo ironworks if you haven’t before.

If you haven’t had enough of winding small roads, it’s recommended to turn towards Vörå in Kaitsor and then ride through Vörå City, turn towards Vaasa along Larvvägen, and then turn off towards Lillkyro. Great roads.

For a shorter detour, you can turn towards Maxmo in Ölis and then ride through Vassor. But be careful—many blind curves and often some light traffic on the road!

### Beach rating

At the beach, there’s parking for more than one vehicle (+). This day, that was plenty. A couple had just been swimming and left as I arrived. Then I had the beach to myself. The beach also has changing rooms (+) and even a dry toilet (+). There’s grass (+) to lie and sunbathe on and sand (+) at the water’s edge. There’s also a fire pit (+), picnic table (+), and trash bin (+).

There’s also a floating dock (+). The depth at the dock edge was just over waist deep (-) and the bottom was mostly clay (-) as far out as I could go. The water looked like Ostrobothnian lake water usually does—very humus-rich and brown (-). But today it was warm (+) and pleasant.

The view? Not much to look at, a lake so big you can’t see much of what’s happening on the other side. Rating: neutral.

*Summary:* Good facilities but the beach is so-so and the water is brown. But the road there is fantastic!

*Recommendation:* Go there. Stop for a swim if it’s really hot, but otherwise just enjoy the road there and back!

*Bonus recommendation:* The beach in Hällnäs is maybe a bit better, and maybe the ice cream kiosk is open there.

Today's ride was 99 km long.

*Rating:* 3.5/5

### Conclusion

I rode 174 km today. When I got home, I was just as sweaty as when I left.
