# New front tyre and a late test ride

The front tyre has had a slow leak, which caused the tyre pressure to drop while riding. Since the tyre had already rolled a bit over 20,000 km, I decided to replace it rather than try to repair it, even though there was still a little tread left. I ordered a tyre and mounting on the rim from [Skurs däck & service](https://www.skurs-dack.fi). All that remained was to remove the front wheel and take it to the workshop.

<!--more-->

When Little Blue is standing on its centre stand, the rear wheel comes up into the air, but the front wheel rests on the floor. I had never removed the front wheel before. I considered using paddock stands under the front forks, but concluded that there could be a large or uneven load on the forks when there is no front axle tying them together. So instead I supported the bike with an axle stand under the oil sump.

![Axle stand under the oil sump](pall.jpg "Axle stand under the oil sump")

While I had the front tyre off, I took the opportunity to scrub the rim and clean the brake disc and brake pads. I also checked the wheel bearings, which seemed fine. I cleaned the brake caliper, the pin, the pads and the disc as well as I could. The brake disc is in acceptable condition and the pads still have a little material left, but sooner or later it will probably be time to replace the pads as well. Really I ought to change the brake fluid too; I haven't changed it at all since the bike came into my ownership.

![Front wheel](dack.jpg "New front tyre and the rim as clean as it is ever going to get")

For some reason I got it into my head to remove the brake disc in order to clean properly - that was probably a mistake. First of all, according to the Haynes manual the bolts really ought to be replaced with new ones once they have been removed. Secondly, the bolts are supposed to be tightened to quite a substantial torque using only a 6 mm hex bit. I reused the bolts anyway, but smeared on a bit of threadlock of my own - if you buy new bolts they come with a locking coating already applied. To be able to tighten to the correct torque I had to get a little creative. The torque wrench set to the correct torque has a 1/2" square drive, but I don't have any 1/2"-to-bit-holder adapters. The solution became the following combination:
* a 1/2" to 3/8" adapter
* a 3/8" to 1/4" adapter
* a 1/4" bit holder
* a 6 mm hex bit

![Combination of adapters](verktyg1.jpg "Torque wrench to 6 mm hex bit")
![Combination of adapters](verktyg2.jpg "Torque wrench to 6 mm hex bit")

Then it was just a matter of tightening to 43 Nm. Or was it? You tighten in a criss-cross pattern and I must have been fairly close to 43 Nm when - then it suddenly became very easy. The weak link turned out to be the 3/8" to 1/4" adapter, which came from some cheap socket set I've had forever. In fact, it's not the first socket or adapter I've broken from that set. Afterwards I tried tightening a little more with my small 1/4" ratchet, but I could no longer budge the bolts. Nor can you really retighten them after applying threadlock. All I can do is hope that the brake disc is sitting as it should and tightened evenly enough not to start warping or anything like that. Next time I need to get a 1/2" 6 mm hex socket.

![Broken combination](verktyg3.jpg "The adapter cracked.")


## Test ride

Once the brake pads were back in place and the front wheel mounted again, it was already getting fairly late. I still decided to take a short test ride, if only to photograph Vaasa's churches.

![Korsholm church](korsholms_kyrka.jpg "Korsholm church")

[Korsholm church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korsholms_kyrka) (sv) serves as the church of the Korsholm parish even though it is located in Vaasa (Old Vaasa). The building used to house the Vaasa Court of Appeal, up until the Vaasa fire in 1852. The old church, [Saint Mary church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korsholms_kyrkoruin) (sv), was destroyed in the fire, while the court building survived.

![Saint Mary church ruin](gamla_vasa_kyrkoruin.jpg "Saint Mary church")

Not all churches are equally beautiful, at least not from the outside. The [Roparnäs church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roparn%C3%A4s_kyrka) (sv), consecrated in 1964, probably belongs among the less aesthetically pleasing church buildings in this country. The [Dragnäsbäck church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragn%C3%A4sb%C3%A4cks_kyrka) (sv) was consecrated in the same decade, but is still nowhere near as ghastly.

![Roparnäs church](roparnas_kyrka.jpg "Roparnäs church")

![Dragnäsbäck church](dragnasback_kyrka.jpg "Dragnäsbäck church")

On Brändö there is the [Brändö church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%A4nd%C3%B6_kyrka,_Vasa) (sv) from 1910.

![Brändö church](brando_kyrka.jpg "Brändö church")

In the city centre there is also the [Holy Trinity Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Trinity_Church,_Vaasa), consecrated in 1862. After the Vaasa fire, the whole city was moved from Old Vaasa to its present location closer to the sea. A new town was built up, as was this church. An Orthodox church was also erected, Saint Nicholas Church, consecrated in 1866. Colloquially this church is sometimes called the "Russian Church".

![Holy Trinity Church](trefaldighetskyrkan.jpg "Holy Trinity Church")

![Orthodox church](vasa_ortodoxa_kyrka.jpg "Saint Nicholas Church")

By the time I reached the last church on this short test ride, it was already quite dark. The [Sundom church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundom_kyrka) (sv) was completed in 1929, then as a side church of the Solf parish. When the municipal boundaries were redrawn in 1973, Sundom became part of the city of Vaasa, while Solf became part of the new municipality of Korsholm. In connection with this the Solf parish was also split, so that the residents of Sundom, and the church as well, were transferred to the Swedish parish of Vaasa.

![Sundom church](sundom_kyrka.jpg "Sundom church")

It was only when I got home that I realised I had missed one church in Vaasa, namely the Vähäkyrö church. Vähäkyrö became part of Vaasa in 2013 and, as an exclave, it still feels quite a lot like a unit of its own. I'll photograph that church another time.

## Result

The new front tyre actually gave a more stable feel than the old one. Perhaps it's just my imagination. At any rate, it holds pressure.

As a downside, it can perhaps be mentioned that the new tyre probably has a slightly larger circumference, so it rubs against the extra extension I fitted to the front mudguard, at least when gravel follows the tyre up towards the mudguard. It doesn't really do any great harm, but it's irritating all the same. I don't know what I should do about it; I think the extra mudguard extension provides a bit more protection from splashes and stone chips.

I also noticed that the left rear indicator has an intermittent connection which means it works sometimes but not always. Sigh. It definitely has to be fixed, since it's a very important safety detail.

I also have problems with the controller for the new Koso heated grips. The heat controller sits right next to the indicator switch, so there are a few wrong button presses now and then. This is especially noticeable in town, where there are many turns and lots of signalling. I'll try rotating the heat controller to a different position, but that will mean it becomes harder to reach and/or the indicator light disappears from view.
