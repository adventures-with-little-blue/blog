# Replaced the head gasket and the water pump

This story begins already last autumn. A few days after my trip to [Tampere](../2025-09-06-tour-tammerfors/), on 11 September to be exact, my wife calls and tells me that the car has a puncture and is standing at her workplace. I grab a battery-powered tyre pump and get on the bike, ride there and inflate the tyre, which holds enough air for her to be able to drive home. Later that evening I get a lift back to pick up the bike. It starts and runs as usual, but when I get home and ride the bike into the garage I discover that coolant is positively pouring out of the water pump. Two mishaps on the same day. And, as it turned out, an abrupt end to the riding season. And the beginning of a fairly drawn-out repair process.

But really the story perhaps starts even earlier, already in the summer. After my [Iron Butt SS1000 attempt](../2025-07-31-ironbutt/) at the end of July, the engine had a strange noise, some kind of whining, at high revs. At first I thought it was something loose vibrating, but it may be that this sound was a symptom of something that would lead to the failure. More about that later.

<!--more-->

![Water pump](vattenpump.jpg "Leaking water pump.")

Because coolant was dripping from the water pump, I assumed that the pump gasket was leaking. So I tried to get hold of a gasket set. As usual, nothing is available locally, so everything has to be ordered with fairly long delivery times. I thought I might as well order it myself, and placed an order with [Lelles](https://www.lellesmc.se) (sv), a Swedish company with a good reputation and short delivery times. Well, it took a few days before they sent the package, and then PostNord took over. After more than two weeks I was informed that I could collect the shipment from the town in Sweden that has the same postcode as my own town in Finland. That was less than ideal: drive 1300 km to collect a gasket set worth 20 €? No thank you. I told Lelles that I wanted my money back and that they could do whatever they liked with the gasket set. (It may be mentioned that Lelles refunded the money _and_ sent the gasket set to me anyway - but by then I had already ordered another one.)

In the meantime I slowly got on with the job of dismantling the water pump. That of course meant that the cooling system had to be drained. And at that point things became less pleasant. When I drained the coolant, I discovered that there was quite a lot of oil mixed in with it. That should not happen. It took a while of thinking before I realised that really the only place where oil can get into the coolant is in the cylinder head - which must mean that the head gasket is leaking! Fixing that problem is no longer exactly a small operation.

![Oily coolant](oljig_kylvatska.jpg "Oil in the coolant - what a mess!")

In any case, I gathered my courage and opened up the cylinders. That in itself is a bit of a project. The fairings have to come off, the tank has to come off, the carburettors and the air filter box have to come off, the exhaust pipe has to come off, the valve cover has to come off, the camshafts have to come off, and the cam chain must not be dropped into the crankcase. There were some burnt-looking marks between the cylinder base and the cylinder head, but to be honest I found no place where I could point and say, "that is where it leaked."

In any case, I ended up ordering a substantial pile of various gaskets and spare parts together with a new head gasket, since most gaskets should be replaced if you open things up. I also took the opportunity to order a new gasket set for the water pump. The only cheaper place I have managed to find for ordering OEM parts is [Webike](https://www.webike.fi), which ships from Japan. So it took a solid 2-3 weeks before the parts arrived.

I also tried, to the best of my ability, to measure how straight the cylinder head was. According to my measurements, the head was warped enough to be outside Honda's tolerance. So the cylinder head had to be skimmed. After some searching, I found a place that does that, [Diesel Test](https://www.dieseltest.fi/svenska/) (sv) in Vaasa. They were quick and professional, and it also did not cost as much as I had feared - they charged 67 € for the job. In any case, they told me that they had not needed to skim off as much as I had measured; the head would apparently have been within tolerance. But now it was straight in any case.

![Freshly skimmed head](nyplanat_topplock.jpg "Freshly skimmed cylinder head.")

Then I put everything back together. Naturally I took the opportunity to check the valve clearance as well (within tolerance), inspect the chain tensioner and so on. So everything ought to be in order now, I hoped. The problem was just that it was the end of October and cold and miserable outside. It is no fun at all to work outdoors, and if I ride a warm motorcycle into my combined office/workshop, it smells of oil for a day afterwards. So the whole project was put on ice for a while, waiting for warmer weather.

![Cylinder head back in place](ventillyftare.jpg "The cylinder head back in place, with shims and valve lifters where they should be.")

![Carburettors](forgasare.jpg "Carburettors being fitted back on.")

So at the beginning of March, when the snow had melted and the sun had finally started warming things up again, the project continued with flushing out the cooling system. First with water, then with vinegar water, and after that with distilled water. But when I started the engine to warm it up, it turned out that the water pump was still leaking, despite the new gasket set. A water pump like that can leak in two places: either at the gasket of the pump housing itself (the one I had replaced), or at the shaft towards the crankcase. The latter cannot be repaired (even though Chinese repair kits are sold for that purpose, albeit without any instructions on how to carry out the repair). So there was nothing for it but to get another water pump. Or rather, not a new one, but a used one - expensive in any case, in fact the most expensive part I have bought for this motorcycle. And naturally it still took over a week before it was delivered, even though I, contrary to my usual habit, paid for express shipping.

![It is still leaking](lacker_annu.jpg "The water pump is still leaking.")

Only now did I manage to put two and two together. The reason the water pump started leaking was that the pressure in the coolant became too high. Water pumps are designed so that in such a case they should leak coolant onto the ground instead of leaking it into the oil sump. The pressure became too high because exhaust gases were leaking from the cylinders past the head gasket into the cooling system at high revs. The sound I had heard in the autumn was probably exactly that - a leaking head gasket. And the reason the head gasket started leaking was probably that I rode poor Little Blue 1800 km in one day on a day when the outside temperature peaked above 30 degrees. Perhaps that was it; at least that is my theory at the moment.

By the very end of March I finally had the whole thing back together. The bike started, but it probably still needed some adjustment (idle, carburettor synchronisation and the usual spring service). The weather was rather lousy again by then, so there was no test ride. We will have to come back to that later.
