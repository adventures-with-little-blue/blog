# A museum trip through Satakunta, Kanta-Häme and Pirkanmaa

I have driven between Ostrobothnia and the Helsinki region countless times, almost always along the main roads. Either via Pori along road 2 or along the motorway to Tampere (E12/road 3). This time I decided to take the time to explore some of the area around and between the major roads. Along the way there are several places I am especially looking forward to: [Puurijärvi and Isosuo National Park](https://www.luontoon.fi/en/destinations/puurijarvi-and-isosuo-national-park), Jokioinen Manor, the Nuutajärvi Glass Village, Emil Wikström's artist home Visavuori, and Vehoniemi Automobile Museum. It will be a mix of nature and museums.

<!--more-->

## A delayed start

Optimistically, I had planned to set off at 6 am, but I could not make myself get up that early. I got going at about 06:45, so I already had something of a scheduling problem from the start. My timetable had been calculated so that I would be home before it got completely dark, and now that became more difficult.

On longer trips it often works out that you have to take the fastest and dullest route through the nearest surroundings so that you then have time to explore areas farther away. So today I headed south along national road 8, all the way down to Noormarkku in Pori. I stopped for fuel in Lappfjärd, but did not even indulge myself enough to get off the bike.

## Noormarkku, Kullaa and Kauvatsa

Noormarkku was previously an independent municipality and since 2010 has been part of Pori. I had “discovered” the Noormarkku ironworks area on an earlier trip [earlier trip](../2025-05-30-raumo/) and had thought about taking another short walk there. To save some of the time I had wasted in the morning, I decided just to ride past. I photographed the church and continued on.

![Noormarkku church](01-norrmark_kyrka.jpg "Noormarkku church.")

The road towards Kullaa is narrow and winding, but unfortunately the asphalt is in rather poor condition. I actually think I have ridden this road before. Kullaa was previously independent and since 2005 has been part of Ulvila. In Kullaa there is the Leineperi ironworks area with Fredriksfors ironworks, but I could not squeeze that into this trip. That will have to wait for another time.

![Kullaa church](02-kulla_kyrka.jpg "Kullaa church.")

The roads are small and the villages are small. The next waypoint was Kauvatsa, previously an independent municipality and since 1969 part of Kokemäki. A cosy little village, but unfortunately Gulf no longer sells fuel there (perhaps they used Teboil as supplier).

## Keikyä

All the way from Noormarkku we had been moving a little north of the [Kokemäenjoki river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokem%C3%A4enjoki). This is one of Finland's largest rivers, with a drainage basin that stretches surprisingly far. From Ähtäri in the north to Riihimäki in the south and almost as far as Päijänne in the east. Now it was time to cross the river, just south of Keikyä church village.

As we rode into Keikyä, we also changed region from Satakunta to Pirkanmaa. Keikyä merged with the municipality of Kiikka in 1981, forming the municipality of Äetsä. Keikyä, like Äetsä, belonged to the former Province of Turku and Pori, that is, Satakunta, but moved together with its neighbours Vammala and Suodenniemi to Pirkanmaa in the regional reform of 1997. In 2009 Äetsä joined with Vammala and Mouhijärvi to form the town of Sastamala.

![Keikyä church](08-keikya_kyrka.jpg "Keikyä church.")

## Punkalaidun

Then back to Pirkanmaa. The road towards Punkalaidun runs alongside the [Punkalaitumenjoki](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punkalaitumenjoki) (fi) almost all the way past the municipal centre, and it was pleasant riding. This is a small municipality whose perhaps most famous attraction is the open-air museum [Yli-Kirra](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talonpoikaismuseo_Yli-Kirra) (fi). The farm in question served as the model for the farm in children's author [Mauri Kunnas'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauri_Kunnas) books about [Koiramäki](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koiram%C3%A4ki) (fi). At least some of the books have been translated into Swedish, where it is called Hundbacken. I did not take the time to walk around the area, but I snapped a photo.

## Humppila and Jokioinen

Time to ride into the region of Tavastia Proper. We also changed sides of road 2, from having ridden on the north side to continuing on the south side. Humppila is perhaps best known as a stopping point along the main road, with Humppilan Lasi as its draw. The church village itself did not feel especially remarkable, but a swimming beach in a small lake right in the middle of the village is not bad at all.

![Humppila church](16-humppila_kyrka.jpg "Humppila church.")

From Humppila also starts the Jokioinen Museum Railway, a narrow-gauge railway that was previously used to connect the industries of Jokioinen and Forssa with the national rail network. I turned in towards the museum at Minkiö station, but noted that the train had just passed and that the place was absolutely crowded. To save time, I continued on towards central Jokioinen. Another time, perhaps. Incidentally, I heard the train whistle a little later when I was strolling around the centre of Jokioinen.

![Jokioinen church](17-jockis_kyrka.jpg "Jokioinen bell tower and church.")

Jokioinen Manor has a long history of both agriculture and industry. Until recently the main building was used by a research unit of the Natural Resources Institute Finland (Luke), but it is now for sale. One of the best-known buildings is the granary. According to legend, the building, which then stood in Humppila, was stolen from the Russian authorities of the time. The soldiers guarding it were plied with drink, then the granary was dismantled, large quantities of brushwood and rubbish were piled onto the spot where it had stood, and the pile was set alight. When the Russians woke up, they thought the granary had burned down. True or not, it is a distinctive building. But the clocks were not showing the correct time.

![Jokioinen manor](19-jockis_herrgard.jpg "The main building of Jokioinen Manor.")
![Jokioinen granary](18-jockis_stapelmagasin.jpg "The granary.")

## Forssa and Tammela

I filled up in Jokioinen and then rode the short distance to Forssa. The old mill area in the centre of Forssa has been redeveloped into a cultural centre with a library, museums, schools, etc. The town centre otherwise was a mix of older and newer buildings, most of them fairly low. In addition, just outside the centre there is a district with older small-house development. All in all, a rather pleasant place.

![Forssa mill](20-forssa_spinneri.jpg "Forssa mill area. Nowadays there are schools, museums, bars and other culture here.")

![Forssa church](21-forssa_kyrka.jpg "Forssa church.")

Tammela lies even closer to Forssa than Jokioinen does. [Tammela church](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tammelan_kirkko) (fi) is old and a little unusual. It is said to be the second longest church in Finland, after Turku Cathedral. It was built in two stages: the first part in the sixteenth century and a substantial enlargement in the eighteenth.

![Tammela church](22-tammela_kyrka.jpg "Tammela church. It does look long.")

I also stopped to look at a memorial to Heavy Battery 15, which fought in the Continuation War and was made up largely of men from Tammela, Forssa and the surrounding area.

![Howitzer in Tammela](23-tammela_haubits.jpg "Memorial to Tammelan Raskas Patteristo 15. The gun is a 150 H/40 howitzer.")

Otherwise Tammela is known for its beautiful nature and its two national parks, [Torronsuo](https://www.luontoon.fi/en/destinations/torronsuo-national-park) and [Liesjärvi](https://www.luontoon.fi/en/destinations/liesjarvi-national-park). Today I did not have time to visit them, but I rode down to [Saari Folk Park](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saaren_kansanpuisto) (fi), an outdoor recreation area by Kaukola ridge a short distance outside the church village. There is also an observation tower on Kaukola ridge, but unfortunately it was closed for the season.

![Saari Folk Park 1](24-saaren_kansanpuisto1.jpg "Saari Folk Park.")

![Saari Folk Park 2](27-saaren_kansanpuisto2.jpg "Saari Folk Park.")

I took the opportunity to eat lunch at Lounais-Hämeen Pirtti in the park. The place is pleasant, but I cannot really recommend the lunch. At a little over 22 € it felt rather expensive, and the food was bland. In addition, the terrace overlooking the lake was occupied by a wedding party, so I had to sit indoors instead of enjoying the summer weather.

![Lounais-Hämeen Pirtti](25-lhpirtti1.jpg "Lunch at Lounais-Hämeen Pirtti in Saari Folk Park.")

Koijärvi was previously an independent municipality, but was annexed to Forssa in 1969 (a small part was transferred to Urjala). In any case it was not a large place. ## Sääksmäki and Valkeakoski

Just after Visavuori we came to the [Sääksmäki Bridge](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A4%C3%A4ksm%C3%A4en_silta) (fi). This was where the old highway ran until the motorway between Helsinki and Tampere opened in 2000. I remember that I myself still had time to drive over this bridge a few times before the new motorway opened.

![Sääksmäki Bridge](47-saaksmaki_bro.jpg "Sääksmäki Bridge.")

Sääksmäki was previously an independent municipality and was incorporated into Valkeakoski in 1973. Finland's largest so-called ancient hill fort, [Rapola hill fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapola_Hill_Fort), lies only a short distance from the old church. Around the fort, Iron Age settlements have been found and dated to almost 2,000 years ago. [Sääksmäki church](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A4%C3%A4ksm%C3%A4en_kirkko) (fi) was completed in the early sixteenth century.

![Sääksmäki church](48-saaksmaki_kyrka.jpg "Sääksmäki church")

Valkeakoski town centre is only 10 km from Sääksmäki church. It was originally Sääksmäki's mill village, but was separated as its own market town as industry developed and later became a town. Today the town is best known for its paper industry, the UPM-Kymmene Tervasaari paper mill and others. We rode to the former paper mill on Myllysaari, Mill Island, which now houses museums. Unfortunately the museums, including the football museum, had already closed for the day.

![Valkeakoski](49-valkeakoski.jpg "Myllysaari, the museum centre in Valkeakoski.")

## Pälkäne

After Valkeakoski we rode through Pälkäne. The most interesting thing there was perhaps Pälkäne's [church ruin](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A4lk%C3%A4neen_rauniokirkko) (fi). The church, completed in the early sixteenth century, did not burn down; it had simply been built on unstable ground and eventually collapsed. The new church was completed in 1839, and the old one was then abandoned. This year they are building a glass roof over the ruin to protect it from the weather. That could be an interesting option for the Korsholm church ruin as well.

## Vehoniemi

After Pälkäne there was only a short distance left to one of the absolute highlights of the trip, [Vehoniemi Automobile Museum](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehoniemen_automuseo) (fi). This is a small museum situated on the beautiful Vehoniemi ridge, just south of Kangasala town centre. The museum originates in the private collections of Olavi Sallinen, but objects owned by others are also on display. The exhibition includes cars, motorcycles, mopeds and various related equipment and paraphernalia. Admission is free, and there is a café in the same building. I took the opportunity to get something to eat while I was there.

![Vehoniemi Automobile Museum](52-bilmuseum1.jpg "The automobile museum.")

![Motorcycle with sidecar](53-bilmuseum2.jpg "German motorcycle with sidecar and hand gear shift.")

One interesting object in the exhibition is a car that the then leader of the Soviet Union, [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev), gave to Finland's President [Juho Paasikivi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juho_Kusti_Paasikivi). It is a [ZIS-110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZIS-110) from the manufacturer later known as [ZIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZiL). The “S” in ZIS stood for Stalin. During Stalin's time, factories, cities and all sorts of other things were named after the dictator. The name ZiS was used for this factory as long as Stalin was alive; afterwards it was possible to rename the factory after something more sensible, in this case its long-serving chief Likhachov. Thus ZiS became ZiL. This eagerness to rename things after whichever despot happens to be in charge still seems to occur in the world, for example right now in the United States.

![Paasikivi's car](54-bilmuseum3.jpg "President Paasikivi's ZIS-110.")

Right next to it there is an observation tower on the ridge with views over the lakes Roine and Längelmävesi. [The last time I was in Kangasala](../2025-09-06-tour-tammerfors/), I was reminded of [Zacharias Topelius'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zachris_Topelius) famous poem _A Summer Day in Kangasala_ - and I cannot help doing so again. What a beautiful landscape! Harjula is admittedly another ridge, but it is surely just as beautiful here at Vehoniemi ridge.

![Vehoniemi ridge observation tower](55-vehoniemi1.jpg "The observation tower on Vehoniemi ridge.")

## East of Näsijärvi

With dinner, museum visit and observation tower done, it was then time to start heading home. It was not quite the shortest route, though. Instead of taking the main road towards Vaasa, I rode more or less straight north, on the eastern side of Tampere and on the eastern side of Lake Näsijärvi. It was mostly forest. I had expected a little more settlement here, considering that the area nowadays belongs to Tampere. I rode through two former municipalities, Aitolahti (I missed Aitolahti old church, blast it!) and Teisko. Aitolahti was incorporated into Tampere in 1966 and Teisko in 1972.

![Teisko church](58-teisko_kyrka.jpg "Teisko church.")

In the village of Velaatta there is the [Maitolaiturimuseo](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitolaiturimuseo) (fi), roughly the milk platform museum, which is called Finland's smallest museum. That may very well be true.

![Milk platform museum](59-maitolaiturimuseo.jpg "Maitolaiturimuseo. Note the cow in the background.")

Then the road runs for a short distance through the region of Central Finland, through the village of Murole. I had planned to stop at the café by Murole canal, but there were so many people and such loud music there that a shy individual like me preferred to continue on. Towards Kuru.

## Kuru, Aure and Kihniö

In Kuru, part of the town of Ylöjärvi since 2009, I filled up and then continued towards Kihniö.

![Kuru church](61-kuru_kyrka.jpg "Kuru church.")

The roads between Kuru and Kihniö are fairly poor, patched and with many potholes and bumps. It did not help that the ride went westwards straight towards the setting sun. At its worst, on some uphill stretch, the sun was only a degree or so above the road surface. A little trying to ride like that.

Roughly midway between Kuru and Kihniö lies the village of Itä-Aure. I had, after all, chosen to ride this road, but... You ride fifty kilometres through dense forest. You hardly see a single house or even a field. Then comes a sign saying Itä-Aure. You pass one house and... then you pass the turning to the church. Turn around, follow a gravel road a few hundred metres into the forest to the church.

![Aurejärvi church](61_5-aurejarvi_kyrka.jpg "Aurejärvi church. A curious mixture of a small wooden church, a rather unusual octagonal bell tower and a stone church gate.")

Then back out onto the road. You pass one inhabited house and two that are not. You pass a road sign pointing to Länsi-Aure. You pass a handful more inhabited and uninhabited farms, and then the dense forest begins again. 25 kilometres to Kihniö. There is a great deal of forest in our country, but it feels as though an unexpectedly large share of it is around the village of Itä-Aure.

In Kihniö we make a wrong turn three times. I cannot see the Android Auto screen because the sun is shining in my eyes, and I also find that the Kurviger app's voice navigation sometimes gives completely incorrect instructions ("turn right" when I should continue straight on) and also simply lags behind a bit ("turn right" only comes once we have already passed the turning point). We end up riding out to the Pyhäniemi outdoor recreation and camping area, even though I had intended to skip it.
![Kihniö church](62-kihnio_kyrka.jpg "Kihniö church.")

![Pyhäniemi](63-kihnio_pyhaniemi.jpg "The Pyhäniemi area in Kihniö.")

When we have crossed the railway, the navigator wants us to turn right onto the gravel road towards the main road. I remember this place from the map, and I am quite sure I asked it to route me via the slightly longer paved road, but Kurviger clearly knows better than I do. This time I ignore the navigator and choose the asphalt. Riding gravel when the sun is shining straight into your eyes isn't so much fun in my view. Once we reach the main road, the sun problem decreases, as we are now travelling in a more northerly direction.

## Homeward

I had planned a few short detours along smaller roads running parallel to the main road. The first was a stretch of the old national road through the village of Koskue in Jalasjärvi (since 2016 part of the town of Kurikka). On the site of a former petrol station stands a [de Havilland Vampire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Vampire) aircraft on display. The Finnish Air Force flew six single-seat Vampire Mk. 52 fighters (VA-1 to VA-6) and nine two-seat Vampire Trainer Mk. 55 training aircraft (VT-1 to VT-9) between 1953 and 1965. The aircraft on display, however, is a Mk. 50 that served in the Swedish Air Force, where it was known as the J 28. The marking VA-7 is therefore not authentic.

![Koskue Vampire](64-koskue_vampire.jpg "de Havilland Vampire on display in Koskue, Jalasjärvi.")

![Koskue church](65-koskue_kyrka.jpg "Koskue church.")

After Koskue I took a short coffee break at Juustoportti in Jalasjärvi. By now dusk was already drawing in properly, so I chose to skip the other planned detours and ride straight home. At dusk one must of course watch especially carefully for wild animals, but otherwise the trip home went well. I have apparently finally learned to dress warmly enough. With fleece and long johns it did not really become cold, even though the thermometer showed only +14 and the speed stayed around 100 km/h for most of the way. I got home without incident.

Unfortunately, however, quite a lot of insects had to give their lives. My windscreen and extra flap keep the insects partly away from body and head, but as the picture below shows, some air and insects still hit the upper part of the helmet. Consequently the noise of the slipstream is also fairly loud. Otherwise I do not have much against the wind, but the insects one would gladly avoid. And in autumn and spring it makes a great difference whether the wind hits straight on the chest or not.

## The route

Towards Pori along national road 8 (E8). To save time, it was the direct route. Then along smaller roads in roughly the same direction as road 2 down to Forssa and Tammela. From there via Urjala, Akaa and Valkeakoski to Pälkäne. Then through Kangasala and on the eastern side of Lake Näsijärvi via Teisko (Tampere) and Murole (Ruovesi) to Kuru (Ylöjärvi) and then through Kihniö out to E12/road 3 towards Vaasa.
