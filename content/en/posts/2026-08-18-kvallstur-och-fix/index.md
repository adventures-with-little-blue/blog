# Evening ride and new indicators

This week there was no ride after Monday's orienteering. Instead, there was a short evening ride on Tuesday. This time we did not go any farther than Replot. On the way back we stopped at Fjärdskär and admired the Replot Bridge. Once back home, it was time to replace the rear indicators and do a bit of maintenance.

<!--more-->

## Prologue

Even though there was no ride after yesterday's orienteering, there is still a short story. After the orienteering I rode into town to buy new indicators for Little Blue. On the motorway a motorcycle police officer was parked along the opposite carriageway, apparently ready to catch speeders. When he saw me, he evidently thought I was going too fast. He gestured with his hand that I should ease off the throttle; he probably had no radar of his own. I nodded and waved.

## Strömsö

Today there was instead an evening ride at short notice. My daughter needed a delivery of some forgotten things to her training session in Västervik, so I jumped on the bike and delivered them. Once there, I thought it was a suitable opportunity to take a detour past Strömsö and Västervik Marina.

![The approach to the Strömsö villa](02-stromso2.jpg "The approach to the Strömsö villa.")

![The Strömsö villa](03-stromso3.jpg "The Strömsö villa.")

Finland's public service broadcaster Yle has for many years aired a Swedish-language TV programme called [Strömsö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Str%C3%B6ms%C3%B6). It has all along been recorded right by Villa Strömsö in Västervik, Vaasa. It is a beautiful old villa whose surroundings are open to visitors. There is also a public bathing beach there.

The expression "It did not go like on Strömsö" naturally refers to the TV programme. On television everything often seems to work out neatly, calmly, quickly and thoughtfully, while reality often offers mistakes, second attempts and larger or smaller problems along the way. The expression originated in Finnish ("Ei mennyt niin kuin Strömsössä") but also spread into Swedish.

## Replot

Then we took a ride out to Replot.

![Replot church](07-replot_kyrka.jpg "Replot church.")

![The old fire station in Replot](08-gamla_brandstation.jpg "The old fire station in Replot.")

![Replot harbour](09-replot_hamn1.jpg "Replot harbour.")

Replot and the nearby villages lie on an island. In earlier times the archipelago villages were fairly isolated; you could only get to the mainland by your own boat or across the ice when it held. The first regular traffic to the island began in 1907, when a boat line started operating between Vaasa and Replot harbour. The passenger boat was replaced by a road ferry in 1952, by which time the necessary road connections had also been built, partly on the mainland as far as Alskat and partly from Replot village out to the then ferry landing roughly where Bernys is today. The road ferry route was initially 2.8 kilometres long and sensitive to ice conditions and weather. In 1975 an embankment road from Alskat out to Fjärdskär was completed, shortening the ferry crossing to 750 metres. In 1997 the ferry was finally replaced by the [Replot Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replot_Bridge) from Fjärdskär to Bernys.

A memorial stone at Replot harbour commemorates the ferry traffic to Vaasa between 1907 and 1946, though despite that later year the traffic probably continued until 1952. From another angle you can see the Replot Bridge. The road ferries were surely also clearly visible from Replot harbour as long as they were in service.

## Fjärdskär and the Replot Bridge

On the way away from Replot we turned in to the rest area at Fjärdskär, next to the Replot Bridge. It is not a large rest area, but it is well equipped. There is a large cooking shelter with a viewing tower, toilets, a kiosk, which was closed, and a bathing beach. Even though it is not an official campsite, there is room for a number of motorhomes, and you could probably also pitch a tent there if needed.

## On the way home

We took our usual route home, via Jungsund, Karperö and Smedsby. As I passed the turn-off for Botniahallen, a motorcycle police officer came riding out from there. It was probably the same officer as the day before, the one mentioned in the prologue. This time I was not going too fast, but I certainly rode extra carefully anyway. Little Blue had broken indicators, and I had also forgotten my driving licence at home, so it was best not to talk to the police. But he was probably more interested in moped riders and other youngsters, and ignored middle-aged men riding old scrap motorcycles.

I took a detour past [Vaasa Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaasa_Airport) and then continued home to the workshop. The little delivery ride ended up being 96 km.

## Replacing the indicators and other small jobs

Then it was time to get on with the evening's tinkering. Already on Sunday I had washed Little Blue and oiled the chain. Because of that, she was fairly clean and pleasant to work on. I tried to [repair the left rear indicator light](../2026-06-24-blinker/) in June. The repair held for a while, but it started acting up again during my trip to the southeast. Now the indicator has stopped working altogether. So I have decided to fit new indicators at the rear. No soldering and messing about this time; I have obtained suitable round female connectors.

![The rear end taken apart for the indicator replacement](15-byta_blinkers1.jpg "The rear end taken apart for the indicator replacement.")

The indicator lights I bought at Motonet were different for the right and left sides. But when I fitted them according to the instructions, the little drain hole ended up pointing upwards. Surely that cannot be right? So I swapped sides on them so that the hole points downwards. I could not see any other differences, and I do not think the light pattern is adapted for right and left. So either there is some feature of the drain hole that I do not understand, should it point upwards, or these no-brand indicators have simply been packaged incorrectly.

![The new indicators in place](16-byta_blinkers2.jpg "The new indicators in place.")

I bought fairly small indicators, considerably smaller than the old ones. The old ones stuck out so far that they bent when I used the saddlebags. I hope there will be less trouble with the new ones. Unfortunately shorter indicators give a poorer light signature to the rear, and other road users have a harder time seeing which way I intend to turn. But at least the distance between the lights is still comfortably above the legal minimum of 18 cm.

![Comparison between the old and the new indicator](17-byta_blinkers3.jpg "Comparison between the old and the new indicator.")

Otherwise the replacement went fairly smoothly. Now I hope for trouble-free signalling for many kilometres to come.

While the bike was standing in the garage anyway, I took the opportunity to make various small adjustments. Among other things, I lowered the headlight beam slightly; I have noticed that it points rather high.
