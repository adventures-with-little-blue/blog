# Kymenlaakso and Eastern Uusimaa

_Day three of our four-day journey in southeastern Finland._

Today Finland's southeasternmost point (can you say that?) is on the programme. The day's expected highlights are Hamina, Loviisa and the Verla mill area. Our planned route also runs through Porvoo, but if time gets short I will have to cut north past Porvoo. The plan is to spend the night in Repovesi National Park, but it is a long walk from the parking area to the campsite, so I am a little uncertain. We shall see.

<!--more-->

The phone alarm went off again at 6:00, though this time on vibration only so the whole tent village would not wake up. After the morning routine and packing, I was once again ready to start Blue at exactly 7 o'clock. I think everyone woke up now. A little payback for yesterday's television viewing...

![Saimaa](saimen.jpg "Saimaa, 06:30.")

## Down to the coast

From Pähäniemi in Taipalsaari we now headed south. First we rode through Lappeenranta again. I photographed [Lauritsala Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauritsala_Church), which is a little unusual - not altogether ugly despite being built of concrete. The former municipalities of Lauritsala and Lappee were forcibly merged with Lappeenranta in 1967, which is why the present city has three large churches within a radius of only a few kilometres.

![Lauritsala Church](villmanstrand_lauritsalas_kyrka.jpg "Lauritsala Church, Lappeenranta. It is a little unusual.")

I refuelled at ABC Viipurinportti. Since this is probably Lappeenranta's largest filling station, they must surely sell cheap fuel. That turned out to be entirely wrong: this was where I bought the most expensive fuel of the whole trip, EUR 2.199/l. Fortunately I only needed a little over 7 litres. Then we continued south along the broad, straight and boring road toward the Vaalimaa border crossing in Virolahti.

Along the road lies the center of the former municipality of Ylämaa. After the Winter War, a small part of Ylämaa municipality was ceded to the Soviet Union, but parts of the almost completely lost municipalities of Säkkijärvi and Vahviala were transferred to Ylämaa instead, so the municipality's area actually grew. Ylämaa merged with Lappeenranta in 2010.

![Ylämaa Church](ylamaas_kyrka.jpg "Ylämaa Church.")

After nearly 50 km on a large and fast main road, there was some gravel-road riding in the municipality of Miehikkälä. From the village of Muurikkala to the municipal center in Miehikkälä church village, there were 12 km of really narrow and winding gravel road. It may well be that people from Muurikkala prefer the detour along the main road, twice as long, when they need to get to the center. In any event, I felt compelled to make the detour to the municipal center so as not to add another municipality "that I only rode through on the outskirts" to the list of visited municipalities.

![Miehikkälä Church](miehikkalas_kyrka.jpg "Miehikkälä Church.")

## Virolahti

From Miehikkälä we continued toward the [Vaalimaa border crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaalimaa) in the municipality of Virolahti. This border crossing was the busiest of all until the Russians both began their full-scale invasion of Ukraine and used refugees as part of their hybrid warfare against the West. Since the end of 2023, all border crossings into Russia have been completely closed to passenger traffic. Before the Russians began the war, considerable investment was made in shops and services in Vaalimaa. There is the large Rajamarket shopping centre, which is still open but does not seem to have very many customers. The extra-luxurious Zsar shopping centre was also built with Russian money, aimed at wealthy Russians who wanted to shop for Western brand goods. Zsar stands empty today, an ugly and grotesque monument to the fact that the Russians' war of aggression also harms Russians themselves - economically and in other ways.

![Vaalimaa border crossing](vaalimaa2.jpg "The Vaalimaa border crossing is as closed as the one at Niirala.")

![Zsar](vaalimaa1.jpg "The super-luxurious Zsar shopping centre. Russian-owned, ugly and closed down.")

From Vaalimaa we continued south toward the village of Kurkela, right beside the border. The newly built border fence was visible from time to time and the road runs really close to it in places. The prohibited border zone follows the roadside in principle all the way through the village. There is a birdwatching tower in Kurkela, and I hoped one might see a bit into Russia from it - perhaps the smoke from some small oil-harbour fire or something? Unfortunately the tower was too low and the surrounding trees too high. To the extent that one could see anything, there was a wooded hill along the horizon.

![Border fence in Kurkela](kurkela3.jpg "Border fence in Kurkela. The border zone follows the road.")

![Kurkela birdwatching tower](kurkela2.jpg "Kurkela birdwatching tower. Saw neither birds nor Russia.")

On the other hand, it was very calm and peaceful in the small parking area by the birdwatching tower. The border zone begins on the other side of the road and the distance to Russia is about 300 metres. The sun was shining and birds were chirping, so I took the opportunity to make some breakfast porridge and brew a thermos of coffee. Only a single car drove past while I was at it.

![Breakfast](kurkela1.jpg "Breakfast being prepared. The border zone starts on the other side of the road.")

After the small disappointment of Kurkela birdwatching tower's inadequacy, I decided to visit the next possible lookout point, the air-surveillance tower in Pyterlahti. This is a replica of the tower that stood here and was used for air surveillance during the wars. It was mainly Lotta women who watched for Russian bombers and reported by telephone if something appeared in the sky. There were a few hundred such towers throughout the country. This time I thankfully saw no bombers, but I did not see much of Russia either - only forest. Otherwise, it was a really fine observation tower.

![Pyterlahti air-surveillance tower](pyterlax1.jpg "Pyterlahti air-surveillance tower.")

![Pyterlahti](pyterlax2.jpg "The forest furthest away is in Russia.")

So I did not see anything exciting on the Russian side of the border in Pyterlahti either. For one final attempt, I rode on through Virolahti church village and out to the coast-guard station in Hurppu. According to my own calculations, this is the southeasternmost point of mainland Finland, if one can put it that way. But this place, too, offered a disappointment - the parking area that had previously been open was now closed behind a fence, and you could not see much of the sea behind it. I was not keen enough to start forcing my way through dense forest or walk across someone's private cottage plot to get a little view toward the Russian side. So, a little disappointed, I had to turn back toward the church village and then continue via Klamila toward Hamina.

![Virolahti Church](vederlax_kyrka.jpg "Virolahti Church.")

![Klamila Church](klamilas_kyrka.jpg "Klamila Church.")

Quite a few place names here along the coast have their own Swedish name, and in some cases the Finnish name is clearly a Finnishized form of the original Swedish word. According to unclear Internet sources, these parts (and also areas eastward in present-day Russian Karelia) had a bilingual population until the 16th century. In the 16th century, the region was subjected to severe ravaging by the Russians, which led to the population simply being replaced and the language border moving westward. Today Pyhtää in Kymenlaakso is the easternmost municipality with a Swedish-speaking population. One can therefore assume that the Swedish place names are rather old.

## Hamina

The narrow and winding King's Road from Virolahti into Hamina is, incidentally, a real gem. Curvy and hilly, with quite good surface. On the last stretch into Hamina I got stuck behind one of the army's trucks, but otherwise it was really enjoyable riding.

Many years ago I attended the [Reserve Officer School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Officer_School_(Finland)) (RUK) in Hamina. There was not much time and/or energy for sightseeing then, so I actually had very few memories of the city itself. I had not been there since my RUK days, so this was really exciting, one of the highlights of the trip.

I wandered around town for a while and admired all the beautiful old buildings. A really pleasant town, actually. I ate a good ice cream in the market square, but walking around in thick motorcycle trousers still became rather hot.

There is now debate over whether it should be repaired or demolished.

## Kotka

Although I was a little behind schedule, I chose to follow the planned route with an extra short detour via Kymi Church and Korkeakoski. That also let me ride past the old fortress of Kymmenegard.

![Kymi Church](kymmene_kyrka.jpg "Kymi Church.")

Almost all of the present city of Kotka was originally Kymi parish and later Kymi municipality. The island of Hogland, then Kotka, then the archipelago municipality of Haapasaari were separated from Kymi, and later the market town of Karhula was created from the central locality of Kymi municipality. [Gogland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gogland) had to be ceded to the Soviet Union in the peace after the Continuation War. Then the trend turned from breakaways to mergers. Over the years, Kotka has incorporated a number of smaller areas from Kymi. In 1974 Haapasaari was incorporated into Kotka, and in 1977 Kymi and Karhula were forcibly incorporated into Kotka.

On the way into Kotka I first made a turn via Langinkoski and the [Imperial Fishing Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langinkoski_Imperial_Fishing_Lodge). A fairly substantial complex for some tsar to visit every ten years and watch someone pull the odd salmon from the rapids. Today it is, naturally, a major tourist attraction.

![Langinkoski](langinkoski1.jpg "The Imperial Fishing Lodge at Langinkoski.")

![Langinkoski](langinkoski2.jpg "View of the Langinkoski rapids in the Kymi River.")

![Langinkoski](langinkoski3.jpg "The Emperor cooked food on a Korkeakoski stove, he did.")

After a ride through Kotka center, I headed for the [Haukkavuori observation tower](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haukkavuori_utsiktstorn) (sv). It was probably my high point (sic!) in Kotka. I like observation towers. There was also an art exhibition in the tower, just right for resting on the different levels on the way up the stairs toward the top.

## Pyhtää

After Kotka we rode on the motorway for a while. The next stop was Pyhtää church village, specifically the medieval [St Henry's Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyttis_kyrka) (sv), built in the 1460s. The Catholic vault paintings that had been whitewashed over for several decades were uncovered during restoration in the 1950s.

![Pyhtää Church 2](pyttis_kyrka2.jpg "Pyhtää's medieval church.")

![Pyhtää Church 1](pyttis_kyrka1.jpg "Interior.")

Pyhtää is, incidentally, the easternmost bilingual municipality, but the proportion of Swedish-speaking residents is steadily declining. The language minority is particularly vulnerable because Pyhtää now belongs to Kymenlaakso, which is otherwise entirely Finnish-speaking. For the Swedish-speaking residents of Pyhtää, proximity to bilingual Loviisa is important. The Swedish-speaking population is also fairly concentrated in the western parts of the municipality, along the border with Loviisa.

From Pyhtää church village we avoided the motorway and continued west along road 170, through [Ahvenkoski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahvenkoski). Ahvenkoski lies at the westernmost branch of the Kymi River, and this is also the boundary between Pyhtää and Loviisa and thus the boundary between the regions of Kymenlaakso and Uusimaa. Between 1743 and 1809, the national border between Sweden and Russia ran at Ahvenkoski, and the place was then an important border crossing.

## Loviisa

Instead of riding straight into Loviisa, we turned south toward the [Loviisa Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loviisa_Nuclear_Power_Plant) on Hästholmen. We were behind schedule, it was quite a ride and on the whole there was not much to see, but I still did not want to miss the chance. There is no visitor centre at the plant, so it was only a few photographs from quite a long distance away. Well, "been there, done that".

![Loviisa Nuclear Power Plant](lovisa_karnkraftverk.jpg "Loviisa Nuclear Power Plant.")

Then into Loviisa. Now the timetable really began to catch up with us. We were over an hour behind schedule and had planned to eat lunch in Loviisa and walk around town a little. Now all the lunch restaurants were already closed and Hesburger was not tempting. So instead I had an emergency lunch consisting of a lunch biscuit and some nuts, washed down with water. And although Loviisa is a really beautiful town, it was quite a short walking tour: the Town Hall, Bonga Castle and the [church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovisa_kyrka) (sv). We will have to visit the town again some other time.

## Askola and Myrskylä

From Porvoo we then headed north toward Askola. After riding mostly along large and straight main roads since Hamina, it was a welcome change to ride the narrow and winding roads that cover most of Uusimaa's "interior". Otherwise the municipality offered nothing much out of the ordinary. Askola was previously bilingual, but is now Finnish-speaking only.

![Askola Church](askola_kyrka.jpg "Askola Church.")

Myrskylä was a similar experience to Askola. Pleasant roads, not much special to look at. One slightly special thing, though, is that Olympic gold medallist [Lasse Virén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasse_Vir%C3%A9n) has a statue in the center of Myrskylä - he is from Myrskylä and still lives there.

![Lasse Virén statue](morskom_lasse_viren_staty.jpg "Lasse Virén has a statue in Myrskylä city center.")

Myrskylä was previously a predominantly Swedish-speaking municipality, but the language majority shifted to Finnish already at the end of the 19th century. The municipality is still considered bilingual, although the share of Swedish speakers is declining and slowly approaching the monolingual threshold, meaning when less than 6% of the population has Swedish as its mother tongue. Swedish-speaking children mainly attend school in Loviisa.

![Myrskylä Church](morskoms_kyrka.jpg "Myrskylä Church.")


## Liljendal and Lapinjärvi

From Myrskylä we continued toward Liljendal. First came a stretch of gravel road with fairly steep curves and lots of loose gravel. Then the surface changed back to asphalt along a really winding little road. Unfortunately the surface was fairly poor, so perhaps this is not a road that can be recommended for pleasure riding. After many kilometres along winding small roads I had actually rather lost my bearings and was only following the navigator. When we reached the church village, I really thought we had come to Lapinjärvi, but that was not the case.

Liljendal with the church village of Sävträsk was formerly a small municipality of its own, but has been part of the city of Loviisa since 2010. The church lies in the middle of the village, and the former municipality was perhaps best known because General Armfelt lived here for a while in the first half of the 18th century.

![Liljendal Church](liljendals_kyrka.jpg "Liljendal Church.")

From Liljendal we then followed the main road, Highway 6, to the church village of Kapellby in the municipality of Lapinjärvi. Lapinjärvi is a bilingual municipality with Finnish as the majority language, while about one third of residents have Swedish as their mother tongue. Lapinjärvi is also the northernmost bilingual municipality in Uusimaa.

![Lapinjärvi Church](lapptrask_kyrka.jpg "Lapinjärvi Church.")


## Iitti

After Lapinjärvi it was time to head north and leave the Uusimaa region. After riding a short way through the former municipality of Elimäki, now part of the city of Kouvola in Kymenlaakso, we turned along road 360 toward the village of Kausala in the municipality of Iitti in the Päijät-Häme region. It was our first visit to this region.

The road was rather monotonous and it was beginning to show that the day had been long. At ABC in Kausala we took a short break to clean the visor, buy coffee and nibble on a biscuit. Then we continued north toward the church village.

![Iitti Church](itis_kyrka.jpg "Iitti Church. The church and church village are 8 km north of Kausala, the municipality's present center.")

Iitti used to be a large parish, but over the years parts were separated into the parishes of Valkeala and Jaala, and then a part of the municipality was ceded to Kuusankoski in 1921. When the railway between Riihimäki and St Petersburg was built in the 1860s, it was routed through Kausala and the church village was left aside. Gradually the administrative center moved to Kausala.

## Kouvola

From Iitti church village we then continued east toward the city of [Kouvola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouvola). Kouvola has grown through municipal mergers. In 2009 Kouvola swallowed its neighbours Anjalankoski, Elimäki, Valkeala, Jaala and Kuusankoski and changed overnight from a small town into one of Finland's larger cities. In 2025 Kouvola had a population of just under 80,000 inhabitants (the twelfth-largest city in Finland), while its land area of 2559 km2 is more than three times larger than the entire Helsinki metropolitan area. It is the largest city in southern Finland by area.

First we rode through Kuusankoski. I had planned a dinner stop here, perhaps at Hesburger if no other options appeared. But once there I did not feel like a hamburger, and besides it was rather late and there was still quite a way to ride. So I photographed [Kuusankoski Church](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuusankosken_kirkko) (fi) and then we continued on.

![Kuusankoski Church](kuusankoskis_kyrka.jpg "Kuusankoski Church.")

The city of Kouvola is sometimes jokingly called Finland's ugliest city. True or not, I actually found nothing worth photographing in Kouvola proper. We rode through the center. There was not much special about it, an ordinary Finnish small town.

Then on to Valkeala. I photographed [Valkeala Church](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkealan_kirkko) (fi) and then continued north.


## Verla

It was quite late, the shadows were growing longer and I was actually beginning to feel a little hungry and a little tired. Now it was time to decide whether we would continue straight north toward the overnight stop or make one more detour to Verla. But just as before, when it came down to it, I could not bring myself to leave out any part of the route, so it had to be Verla. Small winding roads and finally a short stretch of gravel road led to this place.

[Verla Groundwood and Board Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verla_Groundwood_and_Board_Mill) (in Kouvola) is a closed but well-preserved wood-processing industrial site, listed as a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site). The factory closed in 1964 and has been a museum since 1972. Most of the facility was formerly in Jaala municipality, the rest in Valkeala. Today all of it belongs to Kouvola.

Naturally everything was closed when we arrived a little after half past eight in the evening, but I walked around the area and enjoyed the quiet. A beautiful area and surely worth another visit when the museum is open. I also missed seeing the rock paintings that lie only a stone's throw from the mill area.

## Night camp

It was now time, in any event, to find a place to pitch the tent and sort out some food. I had intended to spend the night in [Repovesi National Park](https://www.luontoon.fi/en/destinations/repovesi-national-park), but decided not to. It would have been an almost one-kilometre walk from the parking area to the campsite. Since all my things are packed in saddlebags, a tank bag and top box, with the tent as a separate item, I would probably have had to walk twice to get everything to the campsite. That takes time, both this evening and especially the next morning, when I had a long day's route ahead of me. Besides, it was rather late, which in the worst case could have meant that the campsites were already occupied. So I decided instead to try to find a place to pitch the tent in the wild.

We first rode to [Elving's Tower](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvingin_torni) (fi). A fellow was already grilling food behind his motorhome, so I decided not to disturb him and rode on. I looked at a spot in the middle of the forest along a forest road, but found no properly level place for the tent or a good spot for the gas stove. On the map I found a lean-to shelter along a hiking trail that looked promising.

When I reached the place for the promised lean-to shelter, it turned out that this was where the trail to the shelter began. The shelter itself is along a forest path about a kilometre away. Now I gave up the thought of finding a "beautiful" overnight spot. Right next to it was a small gravel area in front of a mobile-phone mast. It would have to do. Tent up and cooking underway. I pitched the tent and parked Little Blue so that neither could be seen from the little gravel road.

![Tent](talt1.jpg "Little Blue got to stand beside the tent. This place does not look that bad, does it?")

![Tent](talt2.jpg "...if you do not look the other way. We have seen more beautiful campsites.")

Then I prepared dinner consisting of freeze-dried food and what remained of the red wine. It was enough to satisfy my hunger. I ate dinner inside the tent because outside there were mosquitoes and not much to look at. Then it was only a matter of brushing my teeth and crawling into the sleeping bag. It was quite an early night after all; it was not yet 23:00 when I was finished. I woke at some point during the night when a freight train passed on the nearby railway track, but otherwise I slept well at this unpretentious overnight spot.

![Dinner](middag.jpg "Dinner was eaten indoors.")


## Today's route

Although it was only 559 km of riding this time, the day became quite long. The visits to Hamina, Kotka, Loviisa and Porvoo took some time. Planning more time for the visits would have been a good idea, but this was the time budget available to us. And apparently I should not put "optional" parts into a route, because I cannot choose to skip them even when the clock and everything else point to the fact that I should. Skipping lunch and eating dinner very late is not something one should do several days in a row, but today it worked out.
