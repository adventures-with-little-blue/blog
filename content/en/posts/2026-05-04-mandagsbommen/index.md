# Monday Orienteering

This spring I decided to take up a long-dormant hobby — orienteering. A couple of local orienteering clubs organise a training session every Monday evening called "Måndagsbommen". There are courses of varying difficulty levels and on Monday evenings timing is organised with electronic punch cards. I even acquired my own [EMIT](https://emit.no/en/) punch card for the purpose.

Since I haven't managed to get anyone else from the family involved in this form of exercise, it's natural to travel to and from events by motorcycle. The top box is perfect for packing a few things.

<!--more-->

You don't need much equipment for orienteering. I put on training clothes, the motorcycle gear on top. In the top box I pack appropriate shoes, punch card, compass and a water bottle. Maybe also a cap and gloves if it's cold. And a pair of dry socks to put on after the run — feet tend to get wet.

Speaking of wet feet, it quickly turned out that shoes made for road running don't work well in wet forest terrain. After two Mondays the sole had come loose from my faithful old Asics shoes. These shoes have lasted over 25 years, been used for five marathons and for more than 2000 km of running. But two times in the forest was too much. Then I tried an even older pair of shoes (which I had probably used in the forest before) but with even worse results — I reached the finish line missing one sole, and I didn't even notice when it fell off.

![Worn-out shoes](skor1.jpg "A pair of worn-out shoes.")

![Worn-out shoes](skor2.jpg "Another pair of shoes, completely done.")

Now I've invested in a pair of trail running shoes so hopefully they'll hold up better to wet and mossy conditions. My first run in them was today and they were really comfortable. I just had to figure out the trick of tucking the shoelaces under the lacing so they don't come undone all the time. These shoes are also equipped with a moisture-protective Gore-Tex membrane. Although I'm somewhat sceptical about that idea (Gore-Tex keeps water out for a short while but traps moisture for a long time) I could notice that there actually was a difference — my feet didn't get nearly as wet this time.

As is typical for orienteering, each training session is held in a different location. This means that on the way to and from events you can get the chance to explore roads you haven't ridden before. Today was training session number four for the year, this time in Petalax, at the school in the village. The course was quite tricky but still offered the opportunity to choose routes along paths and roads (unlike the first session which offered about zero metres of trail running and the rest through difficult terrain). This time I managed to punch correctly to achieve a valid performance.

![Ready for start](orientering1.jpg "Little Blue at Petalax school.")

When the orienteering is done you can take a little extra spin on the motorcycle. But today it was +6°C with heavy clouds, a fresh wind and rain approaching. With wet training clothes under my riding gear, an extra trip wasn't appealing. But I at least managed to photograph [Petalax church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petalax_kyrka) (sv). Then I rode via the shop to fill up fuel and buy some groceries.

![Petalax church](petalax_kyrka.jpg "Petalax church")

On this short evening trip I passed 1000 km ridden this season. At the same time last year I had ridden nearly double that.
