# Going for a Swim – But Not to the Nearest Beach

The day had been warm, the rest of the family was away, and I felt like going for a swim. It got a bit late before the pets were fed and everything was sorted, so I set off around 16:15. The thermometer showed about 29ºC. Instead of taking the shortest route, I tried to find more interesting roads, but with that temperature I still wanted to stick to faster roads for some cooling wind. The first part of the trip went along familiar roads by the Kyrö river to Vähäkyrö, then Vörå, Ylihärmä, Alahärmä, Voltti, Kortesjärvi, Evijärvi, Kaustinen, and Toholampi.

<!--more-->

In Toholampi I refueled and noticed how incredibly hot it got as soon as I stopped for a moment. Then on to new roads for me. From Toholampi I continued to Sievi. There were quite a few people out in Sievi center. Then towards Ylivieska. After a quick loop through the center, I continued towards Alavieska, on the north side of the Kalajoki river.

Next up was Merijärvi, a small municipality I probably couldn't have placed on the map before. A cozy little village. With just over a thousand inhabitants, Merijärvi only has a comprehensive school, no high school – but there is a ski slope in the village of Pyhänkoski. The Pyhäjoki river flows through Pyhänkoski between bare rock outcrops, right under the bridge on the road between Pyhäjoki and Oulainen – beautiful, a bit unusual, and even a little dramatic. In hindsight, I regret not stopping there for a while, but time was ticking and I was getting more eager for a swim.

![Merijärvi church](merijarvi.jpg)

From Pyhänkoski I went downstream on the north side of the Pyhäjoki river, partly along gravel roads. Quite nice, but the river itself wasn't really visible from the road. In Pyhäjoki center I just made a very short detour from the main road and then headed south on highway 8 towards Kalajoki.

I drove straight past Kalajoki center and continued to Kalajoen Hiekkasärkät, the super nice beach where I intended to swim. I took a short ride through the cottage area just north of the beach and admired the artificial(?) lakes with beautiful luxury villas around them. Then I parked, locked my jacket to the bike with a combination lock, and walked down to the beach.

![Kalajoki](kalajoki0.jpg)

You can look at it any way you want, but this is a world-class natural beach. I honestly can't think of a more beautiful one I've visited. And especially in this weather, there are probably few beaches that can beat it. The beach is just under two kilometers long. There are no buildings or services on the beach itself, it's completely natural (except for the significant wear and erosion caused by tens of thousands of annual visitors). From the beach, a half-kilometer long sand "pier" has formed, and elsewhere there are large areas of very shallow water you can wade through. Beyond the beach, you can see the sea all the way to the horizon. On a beautiful summer evening like this, the sun was slowly approaching that horizon.

![Kalajoki](kalajoki1.jpg)

In most places it's very shallow, but just north of the "pier" I found a spot where it got deep right away. Perfect for someone who might change their mind if they had to wade far through cold water. Now it went so quickly I didn't have time to regret it. It was actually quite cold, around 18ºC, I'd guess. My short swim might not have been quite worth the 350 km I rode to get there, but maybe the swim wasn't the most important part – it was just the symbol and the reason for the trip.

![Kalajoki](kalajoki2.jpg)

After the swim, I spent almost an hour strolling in the shallow water while my swim trunks dried in the sun. It was almost 10 pm when I was back at the bike to continue south. By this time, the water I brought was gone and I was getting hungry, so I took a quick break to buy water and have a snack at ABC. After that, the shadows were already long and the temperature had dropped to around 20, so I put on a long-sleeved shirt over my t-shirt and a tube scarf as well. Then off I went.

Heading south towards Kokkola, I made small detours, first through Lohtaja village on paved roads through the villages of Karhi and Marinkainen, then through the village of Ruotsalo. When planning the trip, this seemed like a good way to avoid the boring highway 8. It probably would have been during the day, but in the growing dusk it was hard to see all the bumps in the road and I mostly worried about elk and deer. I didn't see much of the villages either.

Just before Kokkola, along the north bank of the Perho river, I turned off towards the village of Rödsö. Again, I didn't see much of the village, but at least I got to enjoy a beautiful sunset on the bridge over the river.

![Rödsö](rodso.jpg)

The trip from Rödsö into and through the city of Kokkola went along beautiful roads. I made one more detour to see the sunset, out to Mustakari. Yet another beautiful sunset picture.

![Mustakari](mustakari.jpg)

Then it was really time to head home. The rest of the trip was pretty uneventful. Moderate speed and constantly watching for deer – but luckily I didn't see any. I waved to a few motorcyclists. One of them blinked his turn signal, I don't know if it was a greeting or a warning.

The night was quite warm, so for once I got to experience a longer ride without getting cold at all. I refueled in Oravais and was home about a quarter past one at night. This time the trip was 567 km.

## What did I learn on this trip?

### Gear for warm weather
At +28°C you can just about manage with a helmet, boots, and fairly thick laminated Gore-Tex pants and jacket, as long as the bike is moving. The ventilation holes in the jacket and pants should of course be open. It's also worth keeping the fabric at the jacket's zipper so it doesn't block all the wind, and the sleeves should be fully open so some wind can get up the arms. As soon as the speed is below 80 km/h, it's worth opening the visor (the sun visor protects the eyes). And as soon as you stop, helmet and jacket off.

The worst part was actually my gloves – my hands were too hot all the time. Blue's ugly hand guards are good protection against insects, but on days like this it might be better without them. Thinner gloves that still provide enough protection would be nice.

### Swim gear
I forgot to bring swim shoes. This time I walked to the beach in my boots (and riding pants) – quite a distance. It worked out, but it would have been cooler and more comfortable to leave the boots at the bike.
