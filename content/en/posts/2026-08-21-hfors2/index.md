# From Lahti to Helsinki

Today the goal was to get from my overnight stay in Lahti to work in time for meetings in the afternoon and then the summer party in the evening. Even so, I had not planned an entirely straight route, but continued to avoid motorways whenever possible.

<!--more-->

## Lahti

With the ambition of getting away early, I had set the alarm to ring at a rather unchristian hour. After having a light breakfast and packing my things onto Little Blue, I got away a few minutes after 7, which had been the goal. Just as the weather service had predicted, it was raining, so I had already put on the rain suit.

This was actually my very first visit to Lahti. It is of course impossible to see such a large city in just a few minutes, so I had to choose something representative. First I made a detour past the [Church of the Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Cross_(Lahti)), a modern and rather distinctive church designed by Alvar Aalto.

![Lahti Church of the Cross](01-lahtis_korsets_kyrka.jpg "Lahti Church of the Cross.")

Then I continued up towards the ski stadium. In winter this is naturally one of those places that appears on television time and again, but even in summer the ski jumps and towers are impressive. Lahti is one of Finland's classic winter sports towns, and for that very reason the ski stadium feels almost like a mandatory stop.

![Lahti ski stadium](02-lahtis_skidstadion1.jpg "Lahti ski stadium.")

![Ski jumps](03-lahtis_skidstadion2.jpg "The legendary ski jumps.")

![Tapani Niku](04-lahtis_skidstadion3.jpg "Statue of Olympic medallist Tapani Niku outside the Lahti ski stadium.")

I will probably have to plan a new trip with a day or perhaps even two in Lahti. At the very top of the wishlist for places to visit would be the motorcycle museum.

## Hollola and Kärkölä

From Lahti I continued west towards Hollola. I took a photo of the Messilä ski centre. There must be a fine view from the slopes out over Lake Päijänne.

![Messilä](05-messila.jpg "Messilä ski centre in August.")

Hollola church is a beautiful grey-stone church. It lies quite a long way from the present municipal centre, which has grown up right next to the border with Lahti. After Hollola I continued on towards Kärkölä church village. Kärkölä's administrative centre is the village of Järvelä, which lies by the road between Lahti and Riihimäki. I took the opportunity to fill the tank at the ABC station in Järvelä.

![Hollola church](06-hollola_kyrka.jpg "Hollola church.")

![Kärkölä church](07-karkola_kyrka.jpg "Kärkölä church.")

## Orimattila, Pukkila and Mäntsälä

Then it was onward to Orimattila. Orimattila was the last municipality in the region of Päijät-Häme, which can now be marked as visited. On this trip we actually visited all the municipalities in the region except Iitti. We visited Iitti during our [trip to the southeast](/posts/2026-07-15-sydost3/#iitti).

![Orimattila church](08-orimattila_kyrka.jpg "Orimattila church.")

From Orimattila we crossed the border from Päijät-Häme into Uusimaa and continued to Pukkila. These parts do not offer dramatic scenery, but they do make pleasant riding country: nicely sized winding roads with good surfaces, fields, clumps of forest and church villages at regular intervals. You do notice that we had come to what might be called "crowded Finland" when the typical distance between municipal centres drops from 30-40 km to about 20 km.

![Pukkila church](09-pukkila_kyrka.jpg "Pukkila church.")

From Pukkila it was therefore not far to Mäntsälä. By an elementary school in the church village, the police were sitting in an unmarked van. Lucky that I am in the habit of riding slowly past schools.

![Mäntsälä church](10-mantsala_kyrka.jpg "Mäntsälä church.")

## Järvenpää and Ainola

After Kellokoski we continued to Järvenpää. There was a quick photo stop at the church there, but the main destination was [Ainola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ainola), the home of Jean and Aino Sibelius.

![Järvenpää church](14-traskanda_kyrka.jpg "Järvenpää church.")

Jean Sibelius is the country's most famous composer of all time. His perhaps best-known work is the Finlandia Hymn. The Sibelius family had the house built once Jean Sibelius had become famous and was surely also in a better financial position. They moved in in 1904. Around Lake Tuusula there was a whole artists' colony, and many of the houses now function as museums. Among those who lived there were Aino's brother, the painter Eero Järnefelt, the painter Pekka Halonen, and the artist Venny Soldan-Brofeldt with her husband, the writer Juhani Aho. The poet Eino Leino also visited the area frequently.

## Pornainen and Sipoo

From Järvenpää we crossed the Lahti motorway once more to reach Pornainen. I have to highlight once again that the roads out in the countryside of Uusimaa are fantastic motorcycle roads. Winding, and with almost perfect asphalt. On a Friday morning there was not too much annoying traffic either. The road between Pornainen and Sipoo in particular is very pleasant.

![Pornainen church](20-borgnas_kyrka.jpg "Pornainen church.")

In Sipoo, the old grey-stone church and the new brick church lie only half a kilometre from each other. Unfortunately I could not fit both churches into the same photo; that would have been interesting.

![Sipoo old church](21-sibbo_gamla_kyrka.jpg "Sipoo old church.")

![Sipoo new church](22-sibbo_nya_kyrka.jpg "Sipoo new church.")

## Kerava and Tuusula

Otherwise I feel I would especially have liked to explore Sipoo's small roads, and indeed continue on towards Porvoo as well, but today there was not enough time. Instead I followed Oil Road to Kerava and then on to central Tuusula. Here the route was already beginning to approach the capital region in earnest, and the traffic became ever denser. The town of Kerava feels very much like a suburb. The town has grown quickly and has surely in many ways erased the traces of what was there before. Kerava church may stand as a representative of that, not exactly charming.

![Kerava church](23-kervo_kyrka.jpg "Kerava church.")

By contrast, Tuusula church and the church hill are an oasis in the suburban concrete. The church is beautiful, and the old parish centre still feels fairly intact.

![Tuusula church](24-tusby_kyrka.jpg "Tuusula church.")

![Church hill in Tuusula](25-tusby_kyrkbacke.jpg "Church hill in Tuusula.")

## Vantaa

From Tuusula there were nevertheless a few kilometres of motorway along the Tuusula highway in to [St. Lawrence's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence%27s_Church,_Vantaa) (sv: Helsinge kyrka) in Vantaa. As for the swedish name, one can note that Vantaa, Finland's fourth largest city by population, was called the municipality of Helsinge (fi: Helsingin maalaiskunta) until 1971. St. Lawrence's was the main church of that parish. The area around the church consists largely of old beautiful wooden buildings.

![St. Lawrence's Church](26-helsinge_kyrka.jpg "St. Lawrence's Church.")

![St. Lawrence's Church](27-helsinge_kyrka2.jpg "St. Lawrence's Church was built before the Reformation and was therefore originally a Catholic church. That can be seen in the interior.")

## Arriving in Helsinki

At last I entered Helsinki, or at least the northern and western parts of the city. There was no reason to ride into the city centre this time. I made one final small detour past Huopalahti church to get the obligatory photo of at least one church from every place I had visited. But I could also confirm that riding a motorcycle in the asphalt jungle with all its traffic lights is not especially fun. You mostly just stand and wait.

![Huopalahti church](28-hoplax_kyrka.jpg "Huopalahti church.")

After that it was time to steer towards my employer's Helsinki office. I parked Little Blue, took a quick shower and was then ready for meetings and the evening programme.

![Evening programme](29-anssi_kela.jpg "Evening programme. The evening's main artist was Anssi Kela.")

Otherwise, we rode through the centres of all the municipalities mentioned above.
