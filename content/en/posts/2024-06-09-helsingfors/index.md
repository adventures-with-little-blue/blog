# The First Long Ride

As I write this, it has been more than two years since I made this trip, but because it was my very first long journey on two wheels, it still feels worth remembering. My employer arranged a summer party in Helsinki, and I took the opportunity to ride my motorcycle down to the capital. The topic became relevant again because I am currently sitting here planning a similar trip to a similar event in Helsinki.

<!--more-->

## Day 1: From Ostrobothnia to Espoo and Helsinki

I do not remember exactly how I reasoned my way into riding the bike south. Even by the shortest route, it is a trip of about 900 km. Before this journey, the longest ride I had done was barely 250 km, always starting and ending at home. In total I had probably ridden about 2500 km, so I did have at least some confidence that the bike would hold together. Rain was forecast, and I had prepared for that: rain gear, "waterproof" boots, and Gore-Tex winter gloves, which I also believed to be waterproof. I had packed my things in a waterproof bag, and I had a rain cover for the tank bag.

![Little Blue packed and ready to go](01-startklar.jpg "Little Blue packed and ready to go.")

I took my first break at [Skantz Cultural Centre](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulttuurikeskus_Skantz) (fi) in Karvia. There I drank coffee from a thermos and ate sandwiches. By then I was already fairly cold and had to conclude that I had not brought warm enough clothes. Foolishly enough, I had left the winter lining out of my jacket. You live and learn.

![The entrance at Skantz](02-skantz1.jpg "The entrance to Skantz Cultural Centre.")

![Little Blue parked at Skantz](03-skantz2.jpg "Little Blue parked at Skantz.")

In Vammala, Sastamala, I stopped for a meal at the restaurant Turkin Pippuri. The riding was going well, but it was still a little chilly. The puddles showed that it had rained earlier in the day, but for the moment it was dry.

![Meal break along the way](04-turkinpippuri.jpg "Meal break along the way.")

I continued along roads I had never travelled before, through southern Pirkanmaa and Tavastia Proper. Then, when I reached northern Uusimaa, perhaps around Nurmijärvi, it started to rain. Since I was already a bit cold, I almost welcomed the chance to put on the rain gear in the hope that it would make me a little warmer. And it did, I stopped freezing. But another problem appeared instead. I was navigating with my phone, and the phone was tucked under the transparent plastic pocket on the tank bag. It was a little awkward to follow the map that way, but it worked. Once the rain started, though, I put the rain cover over the tank bag, and it immediately fogged up. I could no longer see anything on the phone screen.

I thought rather cockily that this was no big problem, after all I had lived in Uusimaa... That turned out to be easier said than done when trying to navigate the small roads in Nurmijärvi and northern Espoo. My memory was not exactly serving up all the local details I would have needed either. It took a few wrong turns and a few dead ends before I found my way back to familiar places. In the end I did make it to the hotel, located by [Sello shopping centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sello) in Leppävaara, Espoo.

![Little Blue in the parking garage at Sello](05-sello.jpg "Little Blue in the parking garage at Sello.")

My supposedly waterproof gloves were perhaps a little damp, but otherwise I was fairly dry. The trip had not worn me out too badly either. So after checking in and making a quick change of clothes in the hotel room, I and a few colleagues took public transport to the party venue in Helsinki. Then it was party time well into the night. Though if I remember correctly, I actually took it pretty easy.

![Party atmosphere in Helsinki](06-party.jpg "Party atmosphere in Helsinki.")

## Day 2: heading back home

When I set off for home, the rain was steady. Mostly quite light, but steady all the same. I first rode through central and southern Espoo via places that used to be familiar, places where I had lived or worked and where friends had lived. A great deal had changed since I last moved around in those areas 13 years earlier. New roads and bridges had been built, old roads had been altered, and entire districts had sprung up where there had previously been nothing but forest or fields. I could still orient myself roughly, but at one point I was honestly a little lost.

Once the sightseeing part was over, I began working my way away from the capital, all the time along smaller roads. With the rain cover on the tank bag I still had trouble following the navigator's instructions, so I had to stop again and again. In Lohja I had to take shelter from a heavy downpour under some sort of private garage roof by the road. When the downpour passed, I refuelled and continued towards Somero. The rain gradually eased, and somewhere near the border between Uusimaa and Southwest Finland it finally stopped altogether. By that point my hands and feet were thoroughly wet. The "waterproof" boots were not waterproof. The Gore-Tex gloves were definitely not waterproof. And it was quite cold. I kept the rain gear on all the way home to keep at least a little warm air around my body.

I took a short coffee break at the café by [Alastaro Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alastaro_Circuit). Despite the puddles and the heavy clouds, there were motorcycles out on the track the whole time. In a way that was comforting: if they could do it, then so could I.

![Dark clouds over Alastaro](07-alastaro1.jpg "Dark clouds over Alastaro.")

![View of the Alastaro track after the rain](08-alastaro2.jpg "Motorcycle on the Alastaro track.")

I fuelled up in Säkylä and then continued home via Kokemäki, Noormarkku, Pomarkku, Siikainen, Isojoki, and Teuva. All the way to Pörtom, I avoided the main road. I even had to ride a short stretch of gravel road between Siikainen and Isojoki. By then the roads had dried up, but it was still a little chilly. As far as I can remember, I did not stop even once on the rest of the way home.

## Lessons Learned

Every trip teaches you something new, big or small. These are a few things I remember taking with me from my first long ride:

* You get cold when you sit on the bike for a long time, so you need to dress more warmly than you might think.
* Motorcycle boots are not waterproof if it rains for hours on end.
* Gloves, whether they are Gore-Tex or not, are definitely not waterproof if it rains for hours. Admittedly I had the heated grips on, which reduces moisture resistance, but I have also noticed on later occasions that with enough rain they get wet anyway.
* On the other hand, the rest of my body stayed mostly dry thanks to the rain suit.
* Riding in my rain gear is tiring because it flaps in the wind and creates more drag. On this trip I did not yet have a windscreen.
* Despite the rain, I was surprisingly fresh when I arrived. It seems that Little Blue handles fairly long distances quite well.
* Phone navigation does rather depend on actually being able to see the phone. I decided to buy a phone mount.

All in all, the trip went very well. No major problems, just a few lessons about how to dress. The bike held up, although I think there was already some trouble with one of the turn signals.

I used the [Kurviger](https://kurviger.com) app for the first time and immediately liked the idea of planning the trip in detail beforehand. Nowadays I see the planning itself as an interesting part of the journey. I try to identify all interesting places to visit along the route, pick the most relevant ones within the time budget, and optimise the route. I often make several versions of the route before deciding on the final one.

## The Route

The trip came to a total of 1021 kilometres over two days. That was quite a big leap from having previously stayed within a radius of about 100 km from home. Still, it went well.

I did not decide to seriously take on the [Finland/308](../../finland308/) project until the following year, but I counted all the municipalities I had already visited earlier. So my long-term goal is to ride my motorcycle Little Blue through all 308 municipalities of Finland. This year (2026) I have added further criteria for a visit: I try to ride through the town or municipal centre, or at least some secondary centre, and I try to photograph a church or something similar in every municipality I visit. I did not do that on this trip, so I will have to revisit those places sooner or later. I have already revisited many of them, but not all. The places along the route from Hämeenlinna to Espoo to Oripää are the ones I have not gone back to, whereas in all the others I have later ridden through the centre. Even the municipalities where I only passed along the outskirts remain, for now, on my list of visited municipalities.
