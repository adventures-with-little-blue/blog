# Evening ride before a football match

My daughter had forgotten some equipment at home, so I had to take it to the warm-up before the match. Instead of waiting a good hour for the match to start, I went for a little ride. I rode a short way along the Kyrö river to Vähäkyrö, then to Vörå and on to Maxmo. The question was only whether I would make it back before the match started.

<!--more-->

The weather was beautiful, around +15°C and sunny. The road running along the north side of the Kyrö river has long been my favourite - it is a beautiful road close to home, and an hour is enough for a short ride. Unfortunately, the road surface is getting worse over time, and it is becoming increasingly difficult to steer between potholes and patches. The road from Vähäkyrö to Vörå is in the same condition - it is still possible to ride it, but each month it feels as though more unevenness is rising out of the asphalt. But as I said, in fine weather it works for me.


![Vörå Church](vora_kyrka.jpg "Vörå Church.")

I made a short stop at [Vörå Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%B6r%C3%A5_kyrka) (sv), partly to photograph the church for my photo collection and partly to visit a few graves. The church is currently being renovated. Among other things, the exterior has been repainted in a somewhat different shade. The new cream-white shade with brown window frames actually makes it look nothing like its former self. The previous shade was more greyish white.

![Vörå Church](vora_kyrka2.jpg "Vörå Church and bell tower. The bell tower is painted in the shade the church used to be painted in.")

The oldest part of the church was built in the 1620s, making it the oldest preserved wooden church in Finland. The church was enlarged in the 1770s and has also been rebuilt and renovated several times since then. Now it is being renovated again ahead of the 400th anniversary to be celebrated in October.

![Vörå Church](vora_kyrka3.jpg "The poor box figure at Vörå Church.")

The poor box figure stands beside the cemetery gate. Also notice the small iron ladder leading over the cemetery wall. It now leads straight into a tree, but I remember that as a child it was fun to climb over it (there is at least one more in another place). I am not entirely sure why these ladders were built. Tradition says that the bodies of people who had died by suicide could not be carried through the cemetery gate, but had to be taken over the wall. But perhaps the real reason was simply that it was easier to climb over the wall than open a heavy gate. The cemetery gates also had to be kept shut to keep grazing livestock outside.


From Vörå I continued through Lotlax to Highway 8 at Palvis, then south towards Maxmo. I took a detour via [Maxmo Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxmo_kyrka) (sv) and photographed it as well. The church organ was built by [Hans Heinrich](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Heinrich) (fi), who had his organ-building workshop right there in Maxmo. Heinrich closed the workshop in 2009 and moved elsewhere, but donated the organ museum he had built up to the Maxmo local heritage association.

![Maxmo Church](maxmo_kyrka.jpg "Maxmo Church")

The organ museum is at the [Klemets Houses](https://maxmo.sou.fi/en/theklemetshouses/). Unfortunately, the organ exhibition is open only by appointment, so it can be a little tricky to get there for a look. The Klemets Houses as a whole would certainly be worth a visit, in any case. It is closed for this season now, but perhaps next year?

Then it was time to head for the football match at Ristinummi in Vaasa. The sun was now low enough to make riding in the roughly southerly direction difficult. Otherwise, I would have very much liked to take the road via Vassor village, but the road is narrow and winding, so it is not much fun with the sun in your eyes. I had to take the main road instead.

I calculated that I would make it to the football match just in time. But at Tesse's Café in Vassor, I encountered an obstacle I had not taken into account. Road resurfacing work was under way. I had to wait for a pilot car to continue. It took surprisingly long, and the delay came to nearly ten minutes. So I missed the start of the match.

![Roadworks](vagarbete.jpg "Roadworks. Wait for the pilot car.")

Autumn is definitely here. Since it was a really fine evening, I managed well without the winter lining in my riding gear. It was a little chilly while riding where the road lay in the shade of the surrounding forest, but I never began to feel cold. It was properly chilly during the match, on the other hand, once the spectator stand was in the shade. I sat in full motorcycle gear (well, apart from the helmet), including gloves, and it still felt cold. The ride home was so short that I did not have time to start feeling cold, but much farther would not have worked. It was probably around +7°C when I got home. So before long, it will be time to put the winter linings into both my riding trousers and jacket.

The day's ride came to 112 km, and it was a fine ride. I am still toying with the idea of a somewhat longer day trip this autumn, but I am a little hesitant. The days are getting shorter at both ends. Even though the weather is fantastic for the season, the thermometer is slowly but surely creeping downwards. A day trip below +10°C is already starting to be challenging. Besides, Little Blue is not running quite right. There are still fairly strong vibrations (which I do not think are caused by the tyres). The throttle response also still feels a little "reluctant" - something is not quite as it should be. I will probably have to clean the carburettors again this winter. Well, we will see whether there will be another longer ride. At the very least, it requires a rain-free and beautiful Saturday - there is no other time available.
