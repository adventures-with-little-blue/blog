# From Helsinki all the way home

The party is over and it is time to head home. On this day I only had a roughly sketched plan to follow the road more or less via Tampere back to Ostrobothnia. A couple of possible museum visits were pencilled in, but time and weather would decide the day's route. There were several changes of plan during the day.

<!--more-->

## Quite an early start

I was staying in central Helsinki, but Little Blue had to spend the night in my employer's parking garage. Saturday therefore began with an early breakfast and then continued with taking the train out to work.

![Local train](01-tag.jpg "Local train. No rush hour on Saturday morning.")

![Pitäjänmäki station](02-sockenbacka_station.jpg "Pitäjänmäki station.")

I loaded my things onto Little Blue and then made a few quick adjustments to the day's route. I decided to ride via Kauniainen and Espoo Cathedral and then start heading north along small roads. A little after 8:30 we were on the move, which is not bad at all the day after a party.

I had hardly got going before it started raining. I stopped in Leppävaara and put on the rain suit. But it never amounted to much more than a few drops. ## Nurmijärvi

We continued navigating small and somewhat larger roads through Klaukkala, the centre of Nurmijärvi and on to Rajamäki, still within the municipality of Nurmijärvi.

![Klaukkala church](07-klovskog_kyrka.jpg "Klaukkala church. Modern, but somehow easy on the eye.")

![Nurmijärvi church](08-nurmijarvi_kyrka.jpg "Nurmijärvi church.")

![Rajamäki church](09-rajamaki_kyrka.jpg "Rajamäki church. Built for the staff of Alkoholiliike Ab, completed in 1938. Does the shape of the tower not look a little like a liquor bottle?")

Strong spirits have been produced in Rajamäki since the late nineteenth century. The factory was founded by private entrepreneurs, but ever since the prohibition era the state has always had a finger in the pie.

History of the Rajamäki factory:
  * Founded as Hyvinge Fabriks Ab in 1888.
  * Transferred to Valtion Alkoholiliike ("the State Alcohol Movement"), in 1920 when prohibition began.
  * Incorporated into the state-owned Oy Alkoholiliike Ab, later Alko, in 1932 when prohibition ended.
  * Production and distribution were separated from retail sales to Primalco Ab in 1995.
  * Took the name Altia in 2002 after a merger with the parent company and was listed on the stock exchange in 2018.
  * Merged in 2021 with the Norwegian company Arcus and received the new name Anora.

Today 96 per cent alcohol is produced at Anora's factory in Koskenkorva, Ilmajoki. The spirit is transported by truck to Rajamäki, where it is diluted, flavoured and bottled, among other things under the brand names Koskenkorva and Finlandia Vodka.

Then we rode through Hyvinkää and Riihimäki. It may be noted that even though both towns have quite a lot of suburban character, they also still retain some older houses. The centre of Riihimäki in particular is really quite pleasant. I had been there before, but not with Little Blue.

![Riihimäki church](13-riihimaki_kyrka.jpg "Riihimäki church.")

## Janakkala

From the village of Ryttylä you come to Tervakoski in the municipality of Janakkala. Tervakoski has a large paper industry, but is perhaps best known for the amusement park Tervakosken Puuhamaa. Puuhamaa had already closed for the season. There are not many things that look as desolate as a closed amusement park.

![Puuhamaa](15-puuhamaa.jpg "Puuhamaa in Tervakoski, closed for the season.")

![Tervakoski church](16-tervakoski_kyrka.jpg "Tervakoski church.")

I also took the opportunity to fill up with the cheapest petrol of the trip in Tervakoski, €2.038/l for E95. That may not sound especially cheap, but there have not been many times when I have fuelled at a lower price per litre than that during this riding season.

After that the road led us towards Janakkala church village. A short distance before the church lies St. Lawrence's spring, a spring with fresh water that has existed since time immemorial. The water was clean and clear and, despite someone having thrown coins into the spring, I could not resist taking a sip. Very refreshing.

![St. Lawrence's spring](17-stlarskallan1.jpg "St. Lawrence's spring.")

## Hämeenlinna

The last time we "visited" Hämeenlinna, we had in reality only driven a few kilometres of country road on the very outskirts of the town. This time we drove through the centre. It seems to be quite a pleasant town.

![Hämeenlinna church](22-tavastehus_kyrka.jpg "Hämeenlinna church.")

We also drove past the birthplace of Jean Sibelius. It was here that he was born in 1865 and where he learned to pick out tunes on the piano and scrape at a violin. And then, after a while, he ended up at Ainola in Järvenpää, [where we visited yesterday](/en/posts/2026-08-21-hfors2/#järvenpää-and-ainola).

![Sibelius birthplace](23-sibelius_fodelsehem.jpg "Jean Sibelius' birthplace, nowadays a museum.")

However it happened, I drove past Jean Sibelius' birthplace and likewise Häme Castle, even though both had been potential sights to visit. That probably meant I had decided instead to stop at a third, upcoming destination...

## Hattula

Hattula's old brick church was completed at the end of the fifteenth century. It is the oldest church in Tavastia. Here, just as at practically every other church I passed this Saturday, some sort of ceremony seemed to be going on. It was either weddings or funerals at every church. So I refrained from looking inside, even though it would probably have been worth seeing there too.

![Old Hattula church](25-hattula_gamla_kyrka.jpg "Old Hattula church.")

The newer church, by contrast, looks like a fairly standard nineteenth-century church.

![Hattula church](26-hattula_kyrka.jpg "Hattula church.")

## Kalvola, Viiala and Lempäälä

After the Armour Museum I changed the route somewhat again. I decided to ride via Iittala and then through Viiala. Iittala was the main centre of the municipality of Kalvola, which was merged into Hämeenlinna in 2009. The former municipality of Kalvola forms almost an exclave of the merged town. Only a very narrow strip of land between the municipalities of Hattula and Tammela connects Kalvola to the former municipality of Renko, which was also merged into Hämeenlinna in 2009. At the same time the former municipalities of Hauho, Tuulos and Lammi were also incorporated into Hämeenlinna.

![Kalvola church](33-kalvola_kyrka.jpg "Kalvola church.")

After Iittala I was more or less forced to ride a stretch of motorway towards Tampere. The detour would have been unnecessarily long. I soon left the motorway again to ride into Viiala. Viiala used to be an independent municipality but is now part of the town of Akaa.

![Viiala church](34-viiala_kyrka.jpg "Viiala church.")

It had been less than a year since I rode past the church in Lempäälä, but this time I stopped and took a photo. Here too there was a wedding under way. I rode through, among other places, Lempäälä and Pirkkala during my [Tour de Pirkanmaa](/en/posts/2025-09-06-tour-tammerfors/) last autumn.

![Lempäälä church](35-lembois_kyrka.jpg "Lempäälä church.")

## Ylöjärvi

In front of the town hall in Ylöjärvi stands a sculpture called [Alamökki](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alam%C3%B6kki) (fi), as a tribute to the rock band [Eppu Normaali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eppu_Normaali). Eppu Normaali comes from Ylöjärvi. Besides the fact that this band is one of my absolute favourites in Finnish music, they are also particularly topical right now because they have just completed their farewell tour. In other words, they have just called it a day. May the Alamökki sculpture remind people of their music for many years to come.

![The Alamökki sculpture](39-alamokki.jpg "The Alamökki sculpture.")

Now Blue and I have also ridden through the centre of Ylöjärvi. True, we have already previously ridden just outside the centre, and we have also visited several villages in the present-day municipality of Ylöjärvi, among them Kuru.

![Ylöjärvi church](38-ylojarvi_kyrka.jpg "Ylöjärvi church.")

## Changed plans: home via Karvia and Kauhajoki

To get away from the slow traffic I changed the plan slightly again. Instead of following the main road past Kurikka, Koskenkorva and Laihia, I turned off at Parkano towards Karvia and from there continued via Kauhajärvi to Kauhajoki and then onwards home through Jurva. On this route there is seldom much traffic, and the roads are for the most part in reasonably good condition.

![Karvia church](44-karvia_kyrka.jpg "Karvia church.")

Between Karvia and Kauhajärvi you ride a stretch of gravel road, but it was dry, if not dust-free, so it went well. This road actually runs through Kauhaneva-Pohjankangas National Park. Unfortunately I did not quite have time to ride up and have a look at the bog. That will have to wait for another occasion.

![Kauhajärvi church](45-kauhajarvi_kyrka.jpg "Kauhajärvi church.")

![Kauhajoki church](46-kauhajoki_kyrka.jpg "Kauhajoki church.")

I filled the tank one last time in Kauhajoki, and after that only the final stage remained. The rest of the journey followed fairly familiar roads.

## Route

My long-term goal is to ride my motorcycle Little Blue through all the municipalities and cities in Finland. This day we rode 532 km, in fact exactly the same distance as on day 1 of our trip. We rode through 6 new municipalities today, and in total we have now visited 239 of Finland's 308 municipalities.

New municipalities today:

* Kauniainen (Uusimaa)
* Hyvinkää (Uusimaa)
* Riihimäki (Tavastia Proper)
* Hausjärvi (Tavastia Proper)
* Janakkala (Tavastia Proper)
* Hattula (Tavastia Proper)

Riihimäki, Hausjärvi, Janakkala and Hattula were the last municipalities in Tavastia Proper where we had not yet ridden, so now that region too is completely done. In addition, we also rode through the centres of Nurmijärvi and Hämeenlinna, places I had previously only passed on the outskirts. In Ylöjärvi too we rode more centrally than before.
