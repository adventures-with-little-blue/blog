## To Koli National Park

_Day one of my three-day journey eastward._

### Introduction

Finally it was time for the trip I had planned already a year ago. Last year the trip didn't happen. Already then I managed to acquire some critical equipment, such as saddlebags and a smaller tent. But the wife announced that I didn't have time to go at all and so it was that time. This year I negotiated a three-day reprieve for a motorcycle trip.

<!--more-->

![First day's route](hem-koli.webp)

### Early start

The weather forecast was rather gloomy, but with only these three specific days earmarked for motorcycle adventures, there weren't many alternatives – pack the rain gear or stay home.

![Gloomy clouds in the sky](dystert.webp)

To get some sleep and manage to get up at dawn I fled to the cabin already the day before – the children have a habit of keeping the whole house awake until after midnight. The alarm clock rang at 5:15, then a short car ride home to the garage where the bike was waiting, packed and ready. The first day's stage goal was Lakkala camping site in Koli National Park and not the shortest route either. To manage that distance of almost 700 km and also have time to make some small excursions on the way, it was important to set off in time.

The weather report now actually promised a break in the rain for a few hours so I didn't put on the rain gear immediately. Well, already in Lapua it started raining. I assumed it was a short shower, so I thought I'd push through – it wasn't the best decision. Halfway between Lapua and Alajärvi I had to put on rain gear and boot covers, on top of already wet clothes.

Somewhere there, before I had yet put on the rain gear, I saw a fox in a field, nose to beak with a pair of cranes. Maybe they had young nearby. With the rain pouring down and traffic behind me I didn't stop to see how it all played out. Afterwards I regret it a bit, would have been exciting to see.

### Central Finland

A bit after Alajärvi I entered new territory (for me, that is). Sure I've traveled this road some time before, but hardly as a driver and definitely not with a motorcycle. Kyyjärvi in central Finland was the trip's first municipality I hadn't visited. Kyyjärvi center is quite typical for small municipalities in Finland. There's a main street, along which there's a school, a church, a fire station, a local shop and maybe a gas station and maybe a café or a pub. The town's largest and best-known employer should be the concrete element manufacturer Betset. Their factory looked quite a lot like just a concrete element factory. Nor otherwise did a rainy Kyyjärvi leave any strong impressions on me. Too bad you could barely see the lake itself that gave the town its name.

Here somewhere between Kyyjärvi and Kannonkoski it feels like the landscape changes and the coast's flatlands transition to lake Finland. In Piispala, Kannonkoski, at a scenic rest area next to a lake and a bridge I took a short break and devoured the packed breakfast sandwich with thermos coffee. Right here at Piispala I once participated in Finland's Scouts' big camp [Tervas 90](https://fi.scoutwiki.org/Tervas). The 35 years that have passed, perhaps in combination with a teenager noticing slightly different things, meant that I really didn't recognize myself at all. The only place I managed to identify was an old gravel pit that functioned as a kind of amphitheater for all participants at once during the scout camp. Then the village of Kannonkoski itself wasn't much more special than Kyyjärvi – despite all services not lying along the same main street here.

![Kannonkoski](kannonkoski.jpg)
Piispala, Kannonkoski. Blue has full packing.

On the way out of Kannonkoski I was forced to switch to reserve on the fuel tap. And that was worrying for several reasons. Partly I only had just under 290 km on the trip meter – normally I usually switch to reserve somewhere between 320 km and 340 km. This thus indicated a new record in fuel consumption. Partly I had over 30 km left to Viitasaari and the next gas station and I have actually never tested how many kilometers I can drive on reserve. After some reasoning and deliberations with myself I came to the conclusion that the fuel should last, I refrained from turning back to SEO in Kannonkoski. But at least I slowed down so as not to tempt fate unnecessarily.

Sure the fuel lasted, I arrived without problems at ABC in Viitasaari. I skipped the city island with Viitasaari church anyway and drove straight to ABC. I didn't have the energy to turn around in the rain either, so Viitasaari only got a brief visit through the center, or whatever it should be called. Beautifully situated is the city, certainly worth another visit sometime when it's not raining.

After Viitasaari I continued north toward Pihtipudas. The shortest route to Koli would probably have been to turn off toward Keitele and Siilinjärvi, but motorcycle trips usually aren't about getting there as quickly as possible? Just south of Pihtipudas runs a small side road along a high gravel ridge, surrounded by water on both sides. Very beautiful. The center itself is also beautifully situated, with a stream flowing through the city.

### Northern Ostrobothnia

The next town north is Pyhäjärvi in the province of Northern Ostrobothnia. I turned in just south of the central town toward the old church village - a quiet little village on a point. Unfortunately you couldn't see any of the surrounding lake from the village road. After a short drive along village roads I came to the municipality's main town Pyhäsalmi. There I had a cup of coffee at café Karnevaali.

![Pyhäjärvi church](pyhajarvi.jpg)
Pyhäjärvi church.

### Northern Savonia

Then I turned east toward Kiuruvesi. At the entrance to the city of Kiuruvesi itself lies an orthodox [road icon](https://kiuruvesi.fi/palvelut/matkailu/nahtavyydet/tieikonikappeli/), as a reminder that we have now reached the province of Northern Savonia and the part of Finland where a significant part of the population professes the orthodox faith. A short tour through the city otherwise revealed nothing specifically interesting, so I left the city with the feeling that Kiuruvesi perhaps hides its best sides from the fleeting visitor.

![Kiuruvesi](kiuruvesi.jpg)
Kiuruvesi's road icon.

From Kiuruvesi I headed northeast toward Vieremä instead of straight toward Iisalmi. In Vieremä I couldn't help but take a swing by forest machine manufacturer [Ponsse's](https://www.ponsse.com/en/) headquarters. Then south toward Iisalmi. On the way I stopped at a memorial for the [Battle of Koljonvirta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Koljonvirta). This battle is famous as Sweden-Finland's last great victory over Russia during the 1808-09 war. The retreating Swedish-Finnish army managed to defeat the significantly larger Russian attacking force here. This battle is said to have inspired [Runeberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Ludvig_Runeberg) to the famous poem about [Sven Duva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tales_of_Ensign_St%C3%A5l).

![Monument over the Battle of Koljonvirta](koljonvirta.jpg)
Monument over the battle of Koljonvirta. In the background the closed Koljonvirta mental hospital - if there was ever a haunted place, this is it.

In Iisalmi it was lunch break at lunch restaurant Olvi-oluthalli and then a visit to the brewery [Olvi's factory museum](https://www.olvisaatio.fi/panimomuseo/). However, there was no Denmark-inspired "lunch beer", nor was there any exciting special beer for sale. But when I came out of the museum the rain had actually stopped for a while. I kept the rain clothes on anyway, because I didn't really trust the dark clouds on the horizon.

The next stop was Sonkajärvi. I had planned the [International Bottle Museum](https://www.sonkajarvi.fi/fi/vapaa-aika/kulttuuri/museot-ja-nayttelyt/kansainvalinen-pullomuseo) as an optional destination, but decided to save time for the national park anyway. From Sonkajärvi I drove along a short gravel road down to road 5822, which according to the navigation app should be curvy and fun to drive. Well, it was mostly a bunch of holes in the road. Here I drove through some villages in Lapinlahti municipality anyway, so I guess I can add that to the list of visited municipalities. And now it had actually stopped raining.

![Sonkajärvi church](sonkajarvi.jpg)

In Rautavaara I refueled at SEO. There didn't seem to be very many places to get fuel, because a truck was simultaneously maneuvering up to fill diesel from the passenger car pump – that should take quite a while since passenger car pumps fill much slower than pumps for large vehicles.

### Northern Karelia

The city of Nurmes lies on a high ridge, very beautiful. Again a place that would be well worth another visit when it's not raining. Then onward toward Juuka, where I provisioned at S-Market. The local Alko had exactly _one_ type of small red wine bottles, so it was easy to choose.

![Juuka](juuka.jpg)
Juuka

A glance at the weather app revealed that another heavy rain area was approaching. I decided to chance making it to the tourist shop in Koli National Park before the heavy rain started, so I skipped Juuka center with the few blocks large old city, called "Wooden Juuka".

### Koli National Park

I actually made it to [Koli](https://www.luontoon.fi/en/destinations/koli-national-park) before the worst rains came. It wasn't possible to ride the bike up to the Koli hotel, so I had to park below and take the elevator up. That was also a nice experience. Then I spent half an hour in the shop during the worst rain before I ventured out on a short hike along the "three peaks tour".

The Koli peaks were probably the destination I had most looked forward to on the trip, and sure Koli delivered. The short tour could be hiked even with thick motorcycle clothes. Sure I got a bit sweaty but at the same time it was also fine to sit for half an hour in the light drizzle on a cliff on Paha-Koli and admire the magnificent view. A fantastic place, a wonderful experience.

After the Koli peaks I topped up the tank and bought ice cream at Sale in Koli center. Then several kilometers of gravel road through the national park to the camping site on Lakkala point. It wasn't really intended that you should drive all the way and the last bit was a bit off-road-like, but what do you do when you don't have a backpack to carry the stuff in? Actually you should park a good kilometer away and hike the last bit, but the thought of leaving some loose items unguarded by the motorcycle at a parking lot didn't appeal. Once there I pitched the tent, grilled a sausage and then it was time for night's sleep.
