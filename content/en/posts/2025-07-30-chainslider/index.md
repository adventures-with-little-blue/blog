# Replacing old plastic with new

On the bike's swingarm there is a plastic piece on which the chain should slide, so that the chain doesn't wear on the swingarm itself. Or rather, there should be such a part on the swingarm — in Blue's case there was probably only the memory of such a part. The part in question is called a chain slider; I don't even know what it might be called in Swedish. Anyway, I have now ordered one and planned to install it on the bike. The next day I planned to go out on a slightly longer ride, so you want the bike to be in top condition! More about that in the next post.

<!--more-->

I actually had another thing to test as well — I had just acquired a pair of thin summer gloves, something from Richa. My normally thicker Rev'it gloves have felt terribly warm when temperatures hovered around +30. It doesn't help that I have hand guards on that block away all cooling wind. I still can't be bothered to remove them for just a few hot days in midsummer. Soon it'll be cold and miserable again.

But, to the wrenching. First I removed the chain guard. There wasn't much left of the old chain slider. Just unscrew the small remaining piece and of course save the screws and nuts. I checked for signs of wear because the entire friction surface on the plastic part had been worn away for as long as I've owned the bike. But not the slightest sign of wear. Apparently the CB 500 doesn't have such an angle on the swingarm that the chain would rub along the metal too much.

![Chain sliders](chainslider.jpg)
The new and the old — not hard to guess which is which?

To reach and mount the new chain slider I had to release the tension on the chain, but I didn't remove the chain. It was perfectly fine to fit the slider and then use the old washer and nut. The China-made slider actually fit well. Tighten the chain again, and put the chain guard back on.

![Chain slider in place](monterad.jpg)
New chain slider mounted. Hmm, should I do something about the rust on the swingarm?

Then of course it was time for a test ride. At first the new slider made some noise, but after a while it was back to normal. Apparently it needed to wear in a bit. I rode down to Korsnäs, then through Taklax and past Lake Hinjärv. A very fun road to ride between Taklax and Övermark — twisty and with fairly newly laid asphalt. I stopped for a while at the grill area in Bodbacka and enjoyed the peace and the view over the lake.

![Hinjärv](hinjarv.jpg)
By Lake Hinjärv.

After that Övermark — Pörtom — Petalax and home. 136 km should be enough to conclude that the plastic piece sits correctly? The new gloves did feel cooler than the Rev'it gloves — good. Because the next morning I had, as I said, planned a considerably longer trip, and it's good not to suffer from overheated hands.
