# Kajane Bog Nature Trail

This blog has turned into a diary of what I do on the way home from my orienteering training sessions. This time is no different. After being away for two weeks, it was time again for orienteering training, and thus also my first motorcycle ride in two weeks. This time it was mostly gravel roads and forest roads. I wanted to check out Kajane's nature trail.

<!--more-->

Today there was no difficult C course, so I decided to try the B course. It was considerably longer, but perhaps not directly harder. Most of the control points were in places with clear catching features, meaning it was easy to see if you had gone too far. I found all but one control without having to search too long, yet my time was among the last third of participants—competitors get better at harder courses. My time was around 1:20, which is significantly more than what I usually get on C courses. I thought I did reasonably well with my route choices, but along the way I saw a fox, a white-tailed deer, and mostly no other competitors—perhaps a sign that my route choice wasn't quite at the same level as the others.

![The Parking](alnaset.jpg "Packing up after the orienteering.")

Then I got on the bike and headed along gravel roads and forest roads toward Kajane camp and the nature trail. Along a forest road, several large loose stones had been churned up in the wheel ruts. I made a rookie mistake—I stared at the stone that appeared in front of the front wheel instead of looking at the path _beside_ the stone—and of course drove straight onto an almost volleyball-sized stone. Fortunately the speed was quite low and I hit it on the side, so I managed to stay on the bike. The rim doesn't seem to have taken any damage, but it was a solid impact.

I hadn't actually made any plans for what to do in Kajane, I mostly thought I'd check if it would be worth a visit for hiking at some point. When I arrived it was so quiet and beautiful that I got off and stayed for quite a while. I was of course alone at this place on a Monday evening. The sun was shining and the rather fresh wind blew away all the mosquitoes.

![Cooking Shelter](kajane_vindskydd.jpg "Cooking shelter.")

The actual camp area was really impressive. A fairly large sleeping cabin, a wood-fired sauna (though the well pump seemed broken), a cooking shelter, firewood sheds with dry birch, an outhouse, etc. Everything available to use, as long as you keep things in good order and clean up after yourself.

![Sauna](kajane_bastu.jpg "The sauna building.")

![View over the Bog](kajane2.jpg "View over the bog from the sauna steps.")

I don't know how it happened, but eventually I found myself trudging along the nature trail out over the bog. Across the entire bog, boardwalks have been laid, so the first part of the trail you could walk with dry feet. A bit into the bog there is a shelter.

![The Shelter](kajane_kata.jpg "Shelter out on the bog.")

Then you continue over what appears to be undisturbed bog. Small pines grow up to hip height, but otherwise it's just low vegetation. And the boardwalk protects the bog from hikers' boots, which would otherwise have worn quite heavily on the slowly growing bog.

![The Bog](kajane5.jpg "View over the bog.")

![The Boardwalk](kajane6.jpg "The boardwalk.")

After a short walk over the boardwalk, I came to Lillträsk's shelter. A pair of swans navigated around the water lilies in the lake, but were careful to keep their distance. I couldn't see if they had any young ones with them. The peace was only disturbed by the low hum of Takaneback's wind turbines spinning at good speed in the rather fresh wind. Otherwise the windmills also felt like a somewhat disturbing contrast to the otherwise rather wild nature.

![Lillträsk Windbreak](lilltrasket4.jpg "Lillträsk shelter.")

![Lillträsk](lilltrasket3.jpg "Lillträsk with wind turbines in the background.")

![Jetty](lilltrasket2.jpg "What was once a jetty.")

The second half of the nature trail ran along the edge of the bog. The trail passed by Lisansjö, where a pair of cranes waded about. It's no longer really a lake, but a grass-covered wetland with no visible water surface. Here the bog vegetation grew to waist height, the view of the bog was restricted by low-growing pine forest. No boardwalks were laid and sometimes the boots sank a bit into the wet bog. Sometimes the trail nearly disappeared entirely, so it became a matter of hopping between tussocks toward the next trail marker. All in all, the second half wasn't as special as walking over the bog itself—I would have preferred to follow the boardwalk the same way back across the bog.

![Lisansjö](lisansjon.jpg "Lisansjö.")

When I got back to the camp area, I was quite warm. Because of my poor planning, I had walked around the roughly 3 km long nature trail in motorcycle boots and trousers, instead of changing into the lighter orienteering clothes I had in my top box. You only have yourself to blame. I sat for a while and cooled off in just my underwear before it was time to hop on the bike and head off again.

The way home went across the main highway, then from Svarvar in Malax to Pitkäloukko on the Jurva side, and then via Kolne village back out to the main highway. Between Svarvar and Pitkäloukko there was some more challenging riding. The road was fairly dry, but there were such large stones and holes that it certainly wouldn't have been possible to get through in a regular car. No problem on two wheels though. I was a bit surprised at how much settlement there was on both sides of the municipal border — it's still quite far to the nearest village center. But that's the case everywhere in Finland, if there's farmland, there's also settlement nearby.

It got quite late before I was home again, almost 23:30. But then I had also covered almost 10 km running/walking and 80 km in the saddle.
