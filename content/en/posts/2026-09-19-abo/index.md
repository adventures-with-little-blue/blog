# The western parts of Southwest Finland and Turku

It turned into the last long ride of the season. This time Blue and I headed south towards Southwest Finland and Turku. There were castles and churches, some really pleasant roads, and some boring transport stretches.

<!--more-->

When the school autumn terms begin, calendars quickly fill up and there are not many opportunities to use a full day for a long ride. The days are getting shorter and shorter, so one cannot travel too far in a day. The weather also has to be good, or at least not too horrible. Now a gap appeared in the calendar (albeit with a few conditions), and the forecast promised reasonably suitable weather. Since the days are becoming short at both ends, this is probably the season’s last long ride. Or at least the last long day trip; if everything works out, perhaps another kind of trip can still happen later.

## The transport stretch

To make use of all the available daylight, we started before dawn. I put on quite a lot of clothing: winter linings in the jacket and trousers, long johns and fleece. I could proudly note that I had got away even a little earlier than planned when I left the yard at 6:15. Just a few minutes later, however, I had to return less proudly to collect the sandwiches I had forgotten in the refrigerator. Well, at about 6:25 we were under way, five minutes ahead of schedule.

![Ready to set off](01-startklar.jpg "Little Blue is ready to set off.")

When I started, what is known as [civil dawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawn) prevailed, meaning that the sun was still just below the horizon and its light was spread through the atmosphere. It was perfectly possible to ride in this weak daylight despite the clouds. It was also pleasant to watch it get lighter as the sun rose higher above the horizon. At least until the boundary between Närpes and Kristinestad; then the clouds grew denser, so it felt as though it was getting dark again. One must be extra attentive for wild animals at dawn. Near the centre of Pörtom I saw a fox slipping along beside the road, but no other animals. Traffic was fairly quiet, as could be expected.

![Lapväärtti](02-lappfjard.jpg "Refuelling stop in Lapväärtti.")

Since I had started with only a third of a tank, I took the opportunity to fill up in Lapväärtti. I had thermos coffee and a sandwich in the tank bag, so I resisted the temptation to go in for a cup of morning coffee. Many kilometres remained before the interesting part of the journey would begin. The dark clouds brought occasional drizzle. I assumed that there would only be brief showers of light rain, so I did not put on my rain suit. And for once I was right – it did rain somewhat harder for a while just south of Pori, but it was only a short shower. My riding gear did not get soaked through and would soon dry again in the slipstream.

I was, however, becoming properly hungry, and perhaps for that reason it started to feel a little cold. I took a short break at Luvia church. I could not find a bench, so I ate the breakfast sandwich I had brought and drank thermos coffee standing up. The wind felt properly cold. But once back on the bike, it felt much better; a little breakfast worked wonders.

![Luvia church](03-luvia_kyrka.jpg "Luvia church.")

## Old Rauma

According to my plan, I was to pass Rauma centre, but once there I thought it was worth making a small detour. With its World Heritage status, [Old Rauma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Rauma) is surely one of Finland’s cosiest towns. Although the entire district consists of old, low wooden houses, it is not merely a museum but a living town centre with shops and art galleries, and in fact some people were already sitting on the outdoor terraces of cafés at nine on a cloudy Saturday morning. I will gladly come here again.

![Old Rauma](04-raumo1.jpg "Rauma Town Hall.")

![Old Rauma](06-raumo2.jpg "Old Rauma. The nuclear-energy company Framatome apparently also has its office above the café.")

![Rauma Holy Cross Church](05-raumo_helga_kors_kyrkan.jpg "Holy Cross Church in Rauma.")

## Pyhäranta

From Rauma we rode a short distance along the main road before turning towards the coast again, onto the road to Pyhäranta. We now crossed the provincial boundary from Satakunta into Southwest Finland. Pyhäranta belongs to the [Vakka-Suomi sub-region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vakka-Suomi) – the name of the region is not entirely obvious to the uninitiated.

Pyhäranta is a small municipality that perhaps not very many Finns could place on a map. It lies between Rauma and Uusikaupunki, and the municipal centre of Pyhäranta is by the coast, some distance from the main roads. The municipality’s other built-up area, Ihode, is instead by Highway 8. The church sits beautifully down by the shore.

![Pyhäranta church](07-pyharanta_kyrka.jpg "Pyhäranta church is by the sea.")

![Pyhäranta harbour](07_5-pyharanta_hamn.jpg "Pyhäranta harbour, just below the church.")

On the way from Rauma to Pyhäranta, the roads began to dry, and before we reached Uusikaupunki the road surface was actually completely dry. Not much of the promised sunshine was visible, though.

## Uusikaupunki

Uusikaupunki’s welcoming committee was waiting a little north of town: a police patrol conducting breath tests. It was actually the first time Blue and I had been stopped by the police. There were no comments about my breath, nor about Blue’s suspiciously white headlights (a regular H4 has a yellower or bluer light than Blue’s LED), Blue’s rear indicators that scream “aftermarket”, or our otherwise somewhat scruffy appearance.

We began our sightseeing with a trip past the town’s best-known industries. [Valmet Automotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valmet_Automotive) looked very deserted. The car park was empty, as were all the visible storage spaces at the factory. Valmet Automotive has been a contract manufacturer of passenger cars since 1969. Saabs, Opels, Porsches and Mercedes cars have among others been made here. In total, more than 1.9 million cars have been manufactured here. European car manufacturing is now in crisis, and contract manufacturers are naturally the first to go. The last Mercedes was made in 2025, after which a large part of the staff, nearly 1,000 people, were laid off or made redundant. Production is in any case now being converted to make both Patria and Sisu armoured vehicles. Bus manufacturing also seems to be under way.

![Valmet Automotive](08_1-valmet_automotive.jpg "Valmet Automotive.")

There is a car museum beside Valmet Automotive, but unfortunately it was not open yet. Instead, we rode as far out towards the sea as the country road goes. And there we encountered Yara’s Uusikaupunki plant. The factory, formerly belonging to the Finnish company Kemira, has taken over an entire large island, complete with harbour. When travelling around Finland there are some smells one has to get used to. In the countryside it occasionally smells of cattle or pig manure, and in many towns there is the smell of the paper industry. Here at Yara, however, it distinctly smelled of chemicals. Not strongly or directly irritatingly, but clearly nevertheless.

![Yara in Uusikaupunki](08-yara_uusikaupunki.jpg "The fertiliser manufacturer Yara has an entire island to itself.")

At the deep-water harbour lies the shipyard [Uki Workboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uki_Workboat), which builds connecting ferries, workboats, and boats and vessels for the coast guard and military. Among other things, the yard has built the car ferries _Vikare_, serving [Bergö](../2025-07-20-bergo/), and _Nestori_, crossing [Arvinsalmi](../2026-07-14-sydost2/); we have travelled on both on our earlier adventures.

![Uudenkaupungin Työvene](09-uudenkaupungin_tyovene.jpg "Large boats are built at the Uudenkaupungin Työvene yard.")

Then it was time to take a brief look at the town itself. Like many other places with “new” in their names, Uusikaupunki is not particularly new. The town was founded in 1617, and among other things [Uusikaupunki Old Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nystads_gamla_kyrka) (sv), built in the 1620s, attests that the town has some years behind it. The town centre has many old and beautiful buildings.

![Uusikaupunki Old Church](10-nystad_gamla_kyrka.jpg "Uusikaupunki Old Church.")

![Uusikaupunki New Church](11-nystad_kyrka.jpg "Uusikaupunki New Church.")

![Street scene from Uusikaupunki](12-nystad_gatubild.jpg "Street scene from Uusikaupunki.")

When I set off from Uusikaupunki, my phone did not connect Android Auto to the Aocci. The first time that has happened. I hoped it would sort itself out, so I continued. It did not. I now had no map to look at, but I had both a good idea of the route and the navigator’s voice in my headset. So I found where we were headed. It sorted itself out after rebooting the phone at the next stop.

## Lokalahti

We were now right in the fun part of the journey. Narrow, winding roads run through a rolling landscape. It was almost a little sunny, although it did not get any sunnier than this that day. But the rain held off, and it was actually becoming too warm in the winter lining. The next stop was Lokalahti. Lokalahti has belonged to Uusikaupunki since 1981; before that it was an independent municipality. The centre of the church village was really cosy. We parked in a slightly unsuitable place, and I took the opportunity to peel off my fleece and drink some coffee.

![Lokalahti](13-lokalax.jpg "Blue in Lokalahti. The sun is almost coming out.")

![Lokalahti church](14-lokalax_kyrka.jpg "Lokalahti church.")

Lokalahti was apparently the home municipality of the long-serving MP and multiple minister [Ilkka Kanerva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkka_Kanerva). Kanerva was the politician who served in Parliament continuously for the longest time, from 1975 until his death in 2022. In front of the church stands the memorial “Kohtaaminen”, bearing his name.

![Ilkka Kanerva memorial](13_5-kohtaaminen.jpg "'Kohtaaminen', memorial to long-serving MP and minister Ilkka Kanerva.")

## Vehmaa and Mietoinen

The next place to visit was the municipality of Vehmaa. The village was neither large nor very special, but the church is old and beautiful. [Vehmaa Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/S:ta_Margareta,_Vemo) (sv) was built in the first half of the 15th century. It is in fact the first in a long row of beautiful old grey-stone churches that we will visit today, all among the oldest churches in the country.

![Vehmaa church](15-vemo_kyrka.jpg "Vehmaa church.")

After Vehmaa, we rode towards Mietoinen. The road was so pleasant here that I disregarded the planned route and instead navigated by the road signs and my approximate memory of how the places lay in relation to one another. It incurred a small penalty, as the road gradually became worse. There was then a short stretch of gravel road before we reached Mietoinen. Mietoinen merged with Mynämäki in 2007. As a reminder that it was once an independent parish and municipality, there stands [Mietoinen Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mietois_kyrka) (sv), inaugurated in 1643.

![Mietoinen Church](16-mietois_kyrka.jpg "Mietoinen Church.")

## Askainen

From Mietoinen we continued to Askainen, which was an independent municipality until 2009, when it merged into Masku. The place is perhaps best known for [Louhisaari manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louhisaari_manor), where Finland’s president and marshal [C. G. E. Mannerheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Gustaf_Emil_Mannerheim) was born.

We first stopped at the [church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villn%C3%A4s_kyrka) (sv), where Mannerheim, incidentally, attended confirmation classes. The stone church was built in the 17th century.

![Askainen church](19-villnas_kyrka.jpg "Askainen church.")
![Askainen church](17-villnas_kyrka2.jpg "The bell tower of Askainen church.")

The churchyard also contains the Mannerheim family’s burial chapel. The von Haartman family also has a memorial plaque on the chapel – they must have been good neighbours, one assumes. The von Haartmans owned, and still own, the [Lemsjöholm](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemsj%C3%B6holm) (sv) estate (then in Askainen, now in Naantali).

![Askainen church](18-villnas_kyrka3.jpg "The Mannerheim family’s burial chapel.")

Right next to the church is [Askaisten Ritaripuisto](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riddarparken,_Villn%C3%A4s) (sv), a memorial honouring the Knights of the Mannerheim Cross and Finland’s war veterans. The park has a memorial stone for all 191 recipients of the Mannerheim Cross, Finland’s highest military decoration.

![Ritaripuisto](20-riddarparken1.jpg "Askainen’s Ritaripuisto.")

Five people from the present-day region of Ostrobothnia are included: 26th ensign [Matti Varstala](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matti_Varstala) (fi) (Vaasa), 38th corporal [Eino Wenäläinen](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eino_Wen%C3%A4l%C3%A4inen) (fi) (Vaasa), 102nd sergeant [Arvid Nordin](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvid_Nordin) (sv) (Närpes), 109th master sergeant [Harald Storbacka](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Storbacka) (sv) (Esse) and 132nd captain [Carl-Birger Kvikant](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl-Birger_Kvikant) (sv) (Kokkola).

![Ritaripuisto](21-riddarparken2.jpg "Memorial stone to Mannerheim Cross knight 102, Arvid Nordin.")

We then continued to [Louhisaari manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louhisaari_manor). It was so empty and deserted that we drove past the large car park, ignored the prohibition signs and parked in the disabled bay right next to the manor. All buildings were closed at this time of year, so there was no museum visit. But one may freely stroll around the manor garden and kitchen garden. I took the opportunity to scrump one of Mannerheim’s apples – very good, and actually slightly larger than those growing back home in Ostrobothnia.

![Louhisaari manor](22-villnas_slott1.jpg "Louhisaari manor.")

![Louhisaari manor](23-villnas_slott2.jpg "Louhisaari manor. The family would fit both inside and outside – a good football pitch in the back yard.")

![Louhisaari manor](24-villnas_slott3.jpg "The advantage of touring in September: we could park close by.")

After the walk in the manor park, it was so warm that I decided it was time to remove the winter lining from my riding trousers – although I kept the long johns on. And naturally, just as I was standing in the disabled bay with my trousers down, a couple of ladies walked past. The only people I saw there despite being there for well over half an hour. That is how it goes. Back in the saddle, then, and off towards Naantali.

## Milestone: 30k together

Somewhere along Velkuantie south towards Merimasku, near the municipal boundary between Masku and Naantali, Little Blue and I reached a milestone. We have now ridden [30,000 km together](../2026-09-20-30k-tillsammans/). Approximately, at least. I bought her with 68,387 km on the odometer and it now read 98,387 km. Although, to be completely honest, we lost a few dozen kilometres while the speedometer cable was broken.

## Naantali

Merimasku was an independent municipality until the end of 2008, after which it became part of Naantali. We crossed the high Church Bridge and then quickly visited [Merimasku Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merimasku_kyrka) (sv).

![Merimasku Church Bridge](25-merimasku_kyrkobron.jpg "The Church Bridge in Merimasku.")

![Merimasku church](26-merimasku_kyrka.jpg "Merimasku church.")

Afterwards we went to the president’s summer residence [Kultaranta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kultaranta) to see whether [Alexander Stubb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Stubb) happened to be at the cottage. He did not seem to be, and the car park was not much to look at. The actual “summer cottage” could not be seen from the car park. A few towers and battlements do, however, rise into view from Naantali on the other side of the bay.

![Kultaranta](27-gullranda1.jpg "Kultaranta. Not much to see without an invitation.")

![Kultaranta](29-gullranda2.jpg "The president’s summer residence Kultaranta, seen from the Naantali side.")

In Naantali we first managed to ride against a one-way street and then chose the street where signs said only those with business could drive – but we had business there. We were going to look at the town and [Naantali Abbey Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A5dendal_Abbey). The abbey no longer exists, but the church remains.

![Naantali church](28-nadendal_kyrka.jpg "Naantali Abbey Church.")

Naantali is a beautiful little town, well worth a visit (even if one skips [Moomin World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moomin_World)). On a windy day in mid-September, it was no longer crowded along the quays. I walked around for a while, then decided to eat a pizza at a restaurant with a sea view.

![Naantali](31-nadendal.jpg "Naantali’s waterfront promenade.")

![Pizza](30-pizza.jpg "Pizza with a sea view. The beer was, of course, alcohol-free.")

While I ate, I optimised the route a little. I was already more than 1.5 hours behind schedule, so I would have to cut something out. The sun sets early and the weather service had also promised rain later in the evening. It gives a slightly unpleasant feeling: every moment spent enjoying the outing during the day means longer spent in rain and darkness on the way home. I therefore removed some less important items, but still wanted to visit the day’s main destination (coming shortly) and all the new municipalities I had marked on the map. I cut secondary destinations such as the airports in Turku and Pori. I also replaced some twisty country roads with straight main road for the ride home.

## Raisio

The new, slightly less winding route then took us to Raisio. While its large neighbour Turku has made room to grow by annexing land from surrounding municipalities or swallowing whole municipalities, the opposite has happened to Raisio. On five occasions Turku has taken larger or smaller pieces of Raisio. The result is that the medium-sized town is densely built, with a greater population density than, for example, Tampere, Lahti and Vaasa.

![Raisio church](33-reso_kyrka.jpg "Raisio church.")

![Raisio](34-reso.jpg "The statue is called ‘Church Builders Killi and Nalli’. Raisio is densely built but has green areas between the buildings.")

Then it was time for the day’s main destination: [Turku Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turku_Castle). I have certainly visited before, but such an attraction is worth another visit. It was also interesting to compare it with [Olavinlinna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olavinlinna), which I [visited earlier in the summer](../2026-07-13-sydost1/).

Then it was northward again.

On the way out the navigator repeatedly told me to keep right. And before I knew it, I was on Route 9 towards Tampere instead of the planned route. No great harm done; it was a small detour. But I must once again consider whether I should really use Kurviger – it no longer feels like a good navigation app.

## Rusko, Masku, Nousiainen and Mynämäki

I refuelled in Rusko, after which it was mostly navigation from church to church. First [Rusko Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusko_kyrka) (sv), built at the beginning of the 16th century. The municipality of Vahto merged into Rusko in 2009.

![Rusko church](43-rusko_kyrka.jpg "Rusko church.")

Then on to [Masku Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masku_kyrka) (sv). Construction began in the late 15th century. The municipalities of Askainen and Lemu merged into Masku in 2009.

![Masku church](44-masku_kyrka.jpg "Masku church.")

Then onwards to Nousiainen. This is one of the few municipalities in Southwest Finland that has neither swallowed nor been swallowed by other municipalities. We visited [Nousiainen Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nousis_kyrka) (sv). The parish is one of Finland’s oldest, mentioned as early as the 12th century. The current church replaced an older wooden building. It was probably built at the beginning of the 15th century.

![Nousiainen church](45-nousis_kyrka.jpg "Nousiainen church.")

[Mynämäki Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/S:t_Lars_kyrka,_Virmo) (sv) is another of the many old grey-stone churches in Southwest Finland. The church was built around 1430, replacing an older wooden church. The municipality of Mietoinen merged into Mynämäki in 2007.

![Mynämäki church](46-virmo_kyrka.jpg)

## Laitila

After that we rode north for a while on Highway 8. We first looked at [Laitila Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letala_kyrka) (sv). The grey-stone church was built in the second half of the 15th century.

![Laitila church](48-letala_kyrka.jpg "Laitila church.")

We also took the opportunity to ride via [Laitilan Wirvoitusjuomatehdas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laitilan_Wirvoitusjuomatehdas), the brewery that has made the place’s Finnish name, Laitila, familiar in Finland.

![Laitilan virvoitusjuomatehdas](49-laitilan_virvoitusjuoma.jpg "Laitilan Virvoitusjuoma; looking at it makes one thirsty.")

We then continued north along smaller roads. In the village of Soukainen is [Soukainen St Bartholomew’s Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/S:t_Bartolomaios_kyrka,_Letala) (sv). This church, built in the 18th century, was apparently built as a church rather than converted into one afterwards, as many of the country’s small village churches were.

![St Bartholomew’s Church](50-soukainen_kyrka.jpg "St Bartholomew’s Church in Soukainen.")

It was now slowly beginning to get dark. It also started to drizzle. Knowing rain was on its way, I did not wait for heavy rain but put on the rain suit immediately. It did take a while before the real rain began, but rain gear is preferable to soaking wet riding clothes over the next three hours.

## New Rauma

If Rauma’s old district is called “Old Rauma”, we might as well call the municipalities joined to it more recently “New Rauma”. Kodisjoki was annexed to Rauma in 2007.

![Kodisjoki poor-box figure](51-kodisjoki_fattiggubbe.jpg "Kodisjoki local history museum and poor-box figure.")

![Kodisjoki church](52-kodisjoki_kyrka.jpg "Kodisjoki church.")

The former municipality of Lappi was annexed to Rauma in 2009. With the municipal merger, Rauma became the first place in Finland to have two entries on UNESCO’s [World Heritage List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Finland) (Helsinki can now also claim two World Heritage sites, with [Suomenlinna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suomenlinna) and several buildings in the [Aalto Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalto_Works) series). [Old Rauma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Rauma) is one entry on the list, and in Lappi lies the [Bronze Age burial site of Sammallahdenmäki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sammallahdenm%C3%A4ki). We will definitely visit this World Heritage site at some point. In the drizzle and increasing twilight, however, a forest walk did not seem like a good idea.

![Lappi church](53-lappi_kyrka.jpg "Lappi church.")

## On the way home

From Lappi we headed back towards Highway 8, which we reached in the centre of Eurajoki. We photographed [Eurajoki Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Adolfs_kyrka,_Eura%C3%A5minne) (sv) and could note that we had now left the group of medieval stone churches behind us.

![Eurajoki water tower](54-euraaminne_vattentorn.jpg "Eurajoki water tower.")

![Eurajoki church](55-euraaminne_kyrka.jpg "Eurajoki Church.")

Then it was north along the main road in pouring rain. The rain naturally also made it feel darker. I filled my thermos with coffee and my bottle with water at ABC Tikkula in Pori, and then we took a short turn through the centre.

When we photographed [Central Pori Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Pori_Church), a small collision actually occurred. I was looking in another direction at the time, but heard the bang. An ATV driven by a youth ran into the rear of a car that had stopped for a cyclist. Nobody seemed to have been hurt and they started sorting things out calmly, so I continued.

![Central Pori Church](56-centrala_bjorneborgs_kyrka.jpg "Central Pori Church.")

It was, incidentally, already considerably darker than the church photo suggests. When I got back onto the main road, it was essentially pitch black. The worst rain had stopped, with some drizzle now and then. But the wind had increased, making it difficult to hold a straight line. On the ride north I found no car to tag along behind and did not feel like doing 70 km/h on a 100 km/h road for the whole journey. So it became a long and monotonous ride north in the dark.

Oncoming traffic was not particularly dense, but I did have to dip my lights every other kilometre or so. In the gusty wind I moved a little back and forth in the lane. I wonder what oncoming motorists thought when they saw only one light dancing along the road towards them?

All in all, the ride home was not so terrible. The rain suit kept me dry, and actually the gloves too – I have now learned not to use the heated grips when it is wet; perhaps that helped a little. There was not much new rain afterwards; the road was just miserably wet. My “waterproof” Gore-Tex boots, on the other hand, were soaking wet. But it did not get cold.

Otherwise, there were no moose and nothing else exciting to report on the way home. And we got home, around 21:30.

## Route

My long-term goal is to ride my motorcycle Little Blue through all municipalities and towns in Finland. This day we rode 768 km and passed through 11 new municipalities. We have now visited 250 of Finland’s 308 municipalities.

New municipalities today:

* Pyhäranta
* Uusikaupunki
* Vehmaa
* Naantali
* Raisio
* Turku
* Rusko
* Masku
* Nousiainen
* Mynämäki
* Laitila

We have ridden through Pori quite many times, but this was the first time we actually rode through the town centre.
