# Installing New Heated Grips

My Oxford heated grips, which I installed in September 2024, have not really lived up to expectations. They have been troublesome more or less constantly in one way or another, and most recently the right side, the throttle hand, has barely heated at all, while the left hand has been getting roasted. So I decided to replace them with a pair of Koso HG-13 grips instead.

<!--more-->

The Oxford grips have caused trouble almost the entire time. Since the right and left sides were at different temperatures, I tried replacing the very large and floppy standard connectors inside the headlight housing with new bullet connectors. That seemed to make things a little better. The glue has also come loose twice, with the result that the throttle grip rotates while riding, so I've had to patch that up as well.

![Connectors](oxford_connectors.jpg "Replacement connectors for the Oxford heated grips. It didn't help to move them around.")

Most recently the right grip has only been working at half power. The throttle hand, no less, which has to stay on the handlebar the whole time! I tried changing the power supply connectors, but that didn't help either. The multimeter shows continuity through both grips, but that the right grip has slightly higher resistance. But I found nothing obvious, perhaps there is a loose contact inside the wires or the grip itself. In any case, I had had enough of the whole thing and bought a pair of Koso HG-13 grips instead.

The installation was fairly trouble-free. After removing the tank, the biggest job was stripping off all the tape I had wrapped around the wires for the Oxford grips. Just to be safe, I tested the new grips with an external power source to make sure they actually worked. Worked fine.

![Test](testrun.jpg "Testing heated grips with an external power source.")

Then I worked the Oxford grips off, which was much easier than I had expected. A bit of help from a thin screwdriver and some compressed air and they came off. I cleaned the handlebar on the left and the throttle tube on the right, and then pushed the new Koso grips into place with a little WD-40 as persuasion. Really quite simple, actually. Koso didn't mention anything about glue, and I didn't use any either. We'll see how that works out.

Up until now I had been powering my Aocci Android Auto display from a USB socket, but while I was at it I took the opportunity to replace that arrangement with its own dedicated fixed power supply. Then I taped the cables together into a neat bundle and arranged them under the tank as best I could.

![Installation](installation.jpg "Cable routing in progress.")

## Impressions

So how do the new heated grips feel?

* The controls are integrated into the left grip, which makes the grip feel slightly small, even for my rather small hands. Perhaps that's something you get used to.

* I mounted the left grip so that the light would be as visible as possible, but that also meant the button itself ended up right next to the indicator switch, with the result that the heated grip settings can easily be changed when using the indicators. It is possible to rotate it, of course, but then the indicator light is no longer as easy to see.

### June Update

After a bit more than 1,000 km of experience with the new grips:

* The grips provide even and good heat, and the temperature is adjustable enough for all conditions. Well, it hasn't been anywhere near freezing lately, so we'll have to wait until autumn to judge that properly.

* The size, that the grips are fairly narrow, no longer feels like a problem. At least not with the slightly thinner summer gloves.

* The way I originally mounted the left grip, I bumped the controller and changed the settings almost every time I used the indicator. After rotating the left grip slightly so that the button points upward and the light diagonally forward, it still happens from time to time that I accidentally change the settings, but not as often. But the indicator light is hard to see in daylight. And there is a risk that it will become a problem when I start using the high-beam button later this autumn. I think I'll try rotating the grip the other way instead, so that the button ends up level with the horn button. I never use that one, voluntarily at least.

![Grip](handtaget_roterat.jpg "The grip rotated forward so that the button is not next to the indicator switch.")

* Without glue, the grips seem to rotate slightly over time. Not a major problem, since they can be adjusted back. I haven't experienced the grips giving way during use.

All in all, I'm happy with the investment. Despite the ergonomic problems, it's still better than having to troubleshoot all the time. And frozen hands take some of the joy out of riding, and are a safety risk too.
