## Tikkakoski Air Force Museum

_Day three of my three-day journey eastward._

### Introduction

The trip's first stage was from home to Koli National Park. During day two I made my way from Koli to Lake Virmajärvi in Ilomantsi and then via Joensuu to Häyrylänranta Camping in Konnevesi. Day two offered persistent pouring rain all day until the evening, when it finally cleared up. This third and final day the plan was to visit Tikkakoski Air Force Museum and then head homeward.

### Toward Tikkakoski

This day I woke up in a sun-warmed tent. Most of what I packed up was now dry or almost dry after yesterday's pouring rain. Of the riding gear only the boots were damp (Gore-Tex keeps moisture out for a short while but keeps moisture in for a very long time). And so my first pair of gloves was of course still soaked through, they'll probably be damp well into August. But I had several pairs of gloves with me. I took the opportunity to do some service on the bike before the campsite opened at 9, then paid for the overnight stay, ate breakfast and rode off westward.

### The Air Force Museum

Then it was time for the trip's third highlight – [the Air Force Museum in Tikkakoski](https://airforcemuseum.fi). It's an impressive collection of aircraft and related equipment to be viewed. There were probably most aircraft types that have been used in the air force from the first one-off planes to the jet fighters Saab Draken and MiG 21. To highlight some high points I can perhaps mention a Brewster Buffalo that was lifted from a lake in Russian Karelia and which is shown in the condition it was found, and it certainly feels a bit cool to squeeze down into a Saab Draken cockpit.

### Suddenly summer came

From Tikkakoski I first rode to Uurainen and then down to Petäjävesi. I quickly looked at [Petäjävesi old church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet%C3%A4j%C3%A4vesi_Old_Church), which is one of seven Finnish objects on [UNESCO's World Heritage list](https://www.museovirasto.fi/en/about-us/international-activities/world-heritage-in-finland#Pet%C3%A4j%C3%A4vesiOldChurch). Then I continued the journey westward toward Keuruu. In Keuruu I ate lunch at Pizzeria La Mel, which fortunately served lunch with standing tables and at lunch prices even after 2 PM. Then I refueled one last time for this trip and continued westward toward Haapamäki.

![Petäjävesi old church](petajavesi.jpg)
Petäjävesi old church.

Haapamäki locomotive museum is unfortunately closed, I had read that, but I had to confirm that it was the case with my own eyes. Then I had decided to drive straight to Virtala, but I went the wrong way and ended up on the road south toward Pohjaslahti. No major damage done, I had had this tour as a selectable detour. I now took the road via Ylä-Kolkki to get to drive along Rantalansärkkä ("Rantala bank").

And somewhere there, south of Haapamäki, summer came. Suddenly it felt terribly warm in my riding gear. So on a sandy side road just north of Rantalansärkkä I finally took off my long underwear and removed the winter liner from the Gore-Tex jacket. Until July 9th of this rainy, windy and cold year the winter attire had been completely in place. And when summer finally came, Gore-Tex without winter liner didn't feel quite cozy anymore either, now it suddenly became too warm.

### Homeward

Then onward to Virrat, where I wandered down to the harbor and swimming beach, which was now full of bathing children. From Virrat you can take the steamboat Tarjanne all the way down to Tampere, via lakes, canals and locks. That would be exciting. After that I turned off to [Virrat Heritage Village](https://visitheartfinland.fi/en/kohteet/heritage-village/) next to Herraskoski canal.

Navigation without having a map on the screen offered certain challenges, I didn't always take the road I had intended, but I at least made progress in the right direction and to Kihniö, as planned. In Kihniö there wasn't really much to see so I continued north toward the Peräseinäjoki direction and then to Jalasjärvi. From Jalasjärvi via Kurikka and Jurva and home were then familiar roads.

In total it was 1700 km of riding, mostly on the first day with about 699 km.

Budget-wise the trip was quite manageable. I refueled 68 liters of fuel for 120 €. I ate lunch in three places, drank coffee at three, bought some provisions, paid for camping and breakfast in Konnevesi as well as admission to Tikkakoski Air Force Museum. In total I managed on under 200 €. If, that is, you don't count the new phone and the other purchases (saddlebags, tent, etc) and new spare parts needed for Blue - but who would like to count like that?

## What have I learned?

On the whole the trip went well. The amount of riding was quite suitable, 700 km in a day felt completely fine – even the following day. The biggest problem was choosing between riding or stopping and looking at interesting places. And you have to have time to eat.

Before day two I knew it would rain heavily. I chose between following the original plan with the worst rain or instead steering westward toward better weather. I chose to follow the plan. I don't know if it's worth making such a choice next time. It became more about "checking off" Virmajärvi than any real enjoyment. When it's pouring rain it doesn't appeal to stop so often either, it's cumbersome to get equipment off and on – especially the completely soaked gloves. And the gravel roads (mud roads?) were in places so slippery that I could have fallen.

The first night I rode all the way to the overnight spot in Koli National Park. The second night it turned out it wasn't possible to ride to the intended overnight spot in Southern Konnevesi National Park. It seems to be more of a rule than an exception that you can't drive all the way in national parks. It's a shame – there are many national parks I'd gladly visit with motorcycle, but leaving the bike and carrying saddlebags and tent etc. a kilometer along a forest road doesn't appeal.

### Equipment

The motorcycle held together – for the most part. The swingarm end fell off, but ultimately it didn't affect the journey. I attached the side mirror with tools I brought. And so far I've managed well with the set of tools I have with me.

I thought rain clothes would keep water out – at least not the rain gear I have. A few hours of rain they handle well, but a whole day's pouring rain is apparently too much – I was wet all the way to my underwear.

My boot covers can probably best be classified as worthless. A hole appeared at the shift foot already the first time I used them and this time my Gore-Tex boots became completely soaked despite using the covers. Maybe they protect a little against the boots getting completely dirty, but that's all in that case.
