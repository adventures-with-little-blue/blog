# A dugout, the Levaneva Nature Reserve and 90k!

There were some changes to the children's practice times, so I had time to ride to Suunta Jurva's evening orienteering event in Tainuskylä. Naturally I took the bike. The orienteering course was quite difficult for someone used to flat country, with some high cliffs, fairly steep terrain and overall quite a challenge.

It had rained all day and was still drizzling a little. Against better judgment, I still decided to head home via forest roads. It turned into an evening of muddy and slippery gravel roads, but also magnificent nature and finally a small milestone.

<!--more-->


## The dugout in Tainuskylä

![Muddy forest road](korsu1.jpg "I was quickly reminded that I do not have the right tires for muddy tractor roads. Best to park quickly and walk the rest.")

Only a short distance from the orienteering there is a historical site that recalls a situation which a few years ago felt rather remote, but in the current world situation feels increasingly relevant. Three men decided not to report for service when they were called up by the defence forces during the Continuation War, and were therefore classified as deserters. They built themselves a small dugout, where they hid during the winter of 1942-1943. In March 1943 the authorities learned where the deserters were staying and also that they were armed. On 10 March 1943 the korsu was surrounded, with the result that two of the three deserters were killed. There are different versions of what happened: did they shoot themselves, die in combat against the authorities, or were they executed (illegally) on the spot.

![Entrance and sign](korsu3.jpg "The entrance to the korsu and a sign that commemorates what happened.")

The korsu visible today is not the original one. It was reconstructed in 2019 by Tainuksen Kyläyhdistys. In the [inauguration speech](https://www.vapaussotaeppy.fi/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Puhe-Tainuskyl%C3%A4n-korsulla-15.9.2019.pdf) (fi), Tapani Tikkala recounts the events of 1941-1943.

![The entrance](korsu4.jpg "The entrance with a low door.")

![The entrance](korsu5.jpg "The entrance - it was a low door!")

![Inside the korsu](korsu6.jpg "The korsu interior. Quite cramped for three people.")

Now we really have to hope that we never again end up in a situation where one has to take a stand on whether it is right or wrong for the authorities to force the population to defend the country with weapons in hand. After for many years feeling like something from the past, it is starting once again to feel as though the risk exists. This can happen again and unfortunately perhaps far too soon.

## Levaneva Nature Reserve

![The parking area](levaneva4.jpg "The parking area, Kurjen kierros information board")

From Tainuskylä it is quite a short distance to the Kivi and Levalampi artificial lake and the Levaneva nature reserve. A few kilometres of forest road and you are there. I had previously only turned around in the parking area, but now I decided to walk the short distance to the Maalarinmaa bird tower.

The trails are well trodden. There are well-maintained hiking routes from Rajavuori in the municipality of Laihia via Levaneva and Tainuskylä (city of Kurikka) to Kalaisjärvi (municipality of Ilmajoki), and it is clear that they are used fairly often. On this lightly rainy day, however, I was completely alone, or at least my company consisted almost exclusively of winged friends: gulls, various small birds, wood pigeons, some crane or swan, and a few that I could not identify.

![Bird tower](fageltorn.jpg "The bird tower at Levaneva.")

The view from the bird tower is rather magnificent. The mire stretches several kilometres away into the rainy haze. It should be possible to hike across the mire along a boardwalk, and I want to try that one day. No sounds other than nature's own.

![Levaneva](levaneva2.jpg "View over Levaneva")

![Kivi and Levalampi artificial lake](levalampi1.jpg "Kivi and Levalampi artificial lake")

Back on the motorcycle, I followed the artificial lake's "fill channel". Then came the most challenging stretch: a wet and partly torn-up gravel and mud road, seasoned with topsoil that tractors had dragged along from the fields. Really slippery on my road tires. But I made it out safely to the paved road and then steered for home.

![The channel](levalampi2.jpg "The channel empties into the Kivi and Levalampi artificial lake")


## 90,000 km

On the way home I noticed that the bike's odometer was once again approaching a milestone. To avoid having to witness this event on the main road, I turned off onto smaller roads. I also took the opportunity to photograph the [Malax Church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malax_kyrka) (sv) for my collection.

![Malax Church](malax_kyrka.jpg "Malax Church")

Then it was time for the odometer to change its ten-thousands digit, from 80k to 90k. Almost exactly one year since the [previous ten-thousands milestone](../2025-05-19-80k/). For a motorcycle that is almost 30 years old, 90,000 km is not a lot, on average about 3,000 km per year. A few kilometres have surely been lost over the years. I myself had the cable to the gauge broken for a little over one hundred kilometres. Perhaps previous owners have had similar periods as well. The gauge has no digit for 100k, but I still cannot believe that it would have rolled over and that the bike would actually have done 190k. Not even a Honda... But soon it will in any case be time to roll back to zero, if it has another 10k left in it. Hardly this year yet, but we will see. Today's ride was 121 km, for a short evening orienteering trip. :-)
