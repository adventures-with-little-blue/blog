# Football match and an evening ride

I rode to Seinäjoki to watch my daughter's football match and at the same time took the opportunity to make a short extra ride through Seinäjoki and Ilmajoki. This trip also offered a touch of UNESCO World Heritage through [Aalto Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalto_Centre,_Sein%C3%A4joki) in Seinäjoki, part of the recently approved World Heritage site [Aalto Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalto_Works).

<!--more-->

On the way to Seinäjoki I managed to stay off the main road almost entirely. First I followed the Kyrö river to Ylistaro. A slight disappointment there was that the road had been patched in various places. Admittedly it had been rather full of holes and uneven before as well, but at least on a motorcycle it used to be easier to avoid the old bumps than the new patches. Now this road is no longer nearly as pleasant to ride.

![Kitinoja church](01-kitinoja_kyrka.jpg "Kitinoja church.")

From Ylistaro I followed the main road toward Lapua for a while before turning off toward Kitinoja. Then through the village and straight across the main road toward Munakka and Ilmajoki. From there I turned across the Kyrö river toward the Seinäjoki villages of Aunes and Niemistö. Completely new places to me. There was a short stretch of gravel, but after that it was back to asphalt all the way into central Seinäjoki. That road passes through a tunnel under route 67.

![Football match](02-fotboll.jpg "Football match.")

The match perhaps took a little longer than I had expected. By the time it was time to continue, dusk was already setting in. I decided to do the ride through central Seinäjoki and Ilmajoki anyway, fully aware that it would be dark before I got home.

I have certainly visited Seinäjoki now and then, but in fact I had never wandered over to the city's administrative center, the so-called [Aalto Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalto_Centre,_Sein%C3%A4joki). Here you find the town hall, state offices, a library, a theatre and, perhaps most famously, the church [Lakeuden Risti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakeuden_Risti_Church). Both the buildings and the surrounding area were designed and planned by Alvar Aalto. There really is something special about Aalto's buildings. Even though they are fairly simple and austere, they are aesthetically appealing. This whole Aalto Center is now part of the UNESCO World Heritage site [Aalto Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalto_Works).

![Aalto Center in Seinäjoki](03-sjk_aalto_center1.jpg "Aalto Center in Seinäjoki.")

![Lakeuden Risti](04-lakeuden_risti_kyrka.jpg "Lakeuden Risti.")

![Aalto Center in Seinäjoki](05-sjk_aalto_center2.jpg "Aalto Center in Seinäjoki.")

From the center I continued straight south through the district of Törnävä out to [Seinäjoki Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sein%C3%A4joki_Airport). No scheduled flights use this airport anymore, and hardly any charter flights either. The buildings themselves appear to be in use by Sedu, Seinäjoki's vocational education unit.

![Törnävä church](06-tornava_kyrka.jpg "Törnävä church.")

![Seinäjoki airport](08-sjk_flygstation.jpg "Seinäjoki airport.")

From the airport I turned west toward Ilmajoki. By now it was already getting properly dusky. Originally I had planned to ride home via Koskenkorva and Jurva, but I dropped that idea and instead rode home along the main road via Laihia.

![Ilmajoki church](09-ilmola_kyrka.jpg "Ilmajoki church.")

It was properly dark by the time I got home. The moon was shining behind the trees. All in all, the evening brought an exciting football match and 202 km of riding.
