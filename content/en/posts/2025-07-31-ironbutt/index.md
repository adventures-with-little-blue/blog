# Attempt on the Iron Butt Saddle Sore 1000

### Introduction
The Iron Butt Association's Saddle Sore 1000 (SS1000) challenge requires riding 1000 miles (approximately 1609 km) in 24 hours. The journey must be documented with fuel receipts so that the receipt timestamps show the trip's duration, and the distance traveled is calculated in a mapping program as the shortest distance between the fuel stops. If your ride is approved, you win honor and glory and maybe get a diploma. Mostly, you've probably proven something to yourself, I suppose.

On a very warm day at the end of July, it was finally time for my long-planned first attempt at completing the SS1000.

<!--more-->

### First to Ruka

The first fuel receipt showed 4:12. That was my start time, so the goal was to be back at the same place by 4:11 the next day. I was already 12 minutes "delayed" compared to my plan, but that wasn't a big problem. Everything that might be needed for a day on the motorcycle was packed and with me. In the tank bag were extra gloves, first aid kit, a sandwich, a thermos of coffee and water, in the top box were more food, rain gear, towel, swim shorts and a fleece. I always have tools in the box under the seat. On my back I had a hydration pack with 3 liters of water and a drinking tube.

![Start fueling](start.jpg)

The next planned fuel stop was Haapajärvi, so I headed toward Vähäkyrö. Despite wanting to start putting kilometers behind me, I couldn't resist taking the slightly slower road on the north side of the Kyrö river — my favorite loop, simply. Then onward through Vörå and Ylihärmä. In Härmä, just before Härmä Spa, the sun rose for the first time above the treetops. It had been somewhat dawn before, but from now on it started to be real daylight.

Then onward through (or at least just outside) Kauhava, Kortesjärvi, Evijärvi, Halsua, Lestijärvi and Haapajärvi. I took a short break and drank morning coffee from the thermos in Reisjärvi. Then I refueled at ABC in Haapajärvi. I got a blank receipt, but planned to use the electronic receipts in the documentation anyway.

![Morning coffee](reisjarvi.jpg)

From Haapajärvi I continued toward Kärsämäki, then the main road via Pyhäntä to Kajaani. Without even having to put my foot down, I continued northward through Ristijärvi and Hyrynsalmi toward the next fuel stop in Suomussalmi. The route consists two-thirds of 100 km/h roads, except in Kajaani there was barely any traffic and the riding was excellent. It really flowed well, simply. A bit too well, even, but we'll come back to that later.

It's quite sparsely populated in these parts, you don't see much settlement between the scattered communities. Quite beautiful landscape, but for the most part, just forest. Worth mentioning at least Seitenjärvi between Ristijärvi and Hyrynsalmi. Some summer cottages along beautiful, fairly high shores. The road runs a bit along an embankment straight across the lake. Beautiful.

Outside Hyrynsalmi were the first signs that the reindeer herding area begins and sure enough, just outside the center of Hyrynsalmi, on the main road on the bridge over Siltasalmi, I met the first reindeer. They didn't bother to watch out for cars and they weren't in a hurry.

A bit before Suomussalmi came some heavy raindrops and it looked really threatening, but I managed to just miss most of the thunderstorm — no need to put on the rain gear. Arriving in Suomussalmi I fueled and lightened the clothing. By this time it was over 25 degrees warm, so now it was fine with just a t-shirt under the jacket and the thin summer gloves. I also tried to fan open the jacket flaps away from the zipper so that some air would come in that way too.

North of Suomussalmi the road is actually quite long and monotonous. I continued straight past Kuusamo. Some reindeer again, they seemed to prefer being near settlements, as so far on this trip I had only seen them near communities.

Then I turned up to Ruka village for a selfie (been there, done that, so to speak). There were actually loads of people there, in midsummer. The buildings resemble an alpine village — a bit like Levi but maybe a bit smaller. I'd gladly visit the place in winter too.

### Then onward to Levi

From Ruka village down to the main road again and onward toward Kemijärvi. In Suomu the road crosses the Arctic Circle. I stopped briefly and photographed the Arctic Circle monument. Then I rode straight to ABC in Kemijärvi. It should be mentioned, by the way, that the road from the east over the bridge into Kemijärvi center gives quite a nice view of the city's skyline (mainly the church then).

In Kemijärvi I did a quick service on the bike, i.e., greased the chain and tightened the side mirror nut that had come loose a bit. Then I had the day's lunch, which turned out to be perhaps even more tasteless than the usual food you get at this letter chain. But when the coffee was finished and the helmet visor cleaned in the toilet, it was just time to throw myself back in the saddle.

Just north of Kemijärvi, on the way toward Sodankylä, it was time for the day's most tedious roadwork. 35 km of road had been equipped with signs and lower speed limits. Then I had to wait for a pilot car for over 10 minutes and crawl forward at 30 km/h for almost 10 km. All because the road workers were doing paving work on a few hundred meter stretch of road. It was quite hot sitting and waiting with full gear in almost 30 degree heat — while most cars in the queue were of course running their ACs at maximum power.

Before the roadwork was even finished, I turned off the Pelkosenniemi road toward Pyhä-Luosto. Pyhätunturi is a really impressive mountain, it would probably be worth a visit for hiking or skiing.

![Pyhätunturi](pyha.jpg)
Pyhätunturi

In Luosto I saw a sign about a bathing beach. I drove past at first but then changed my mind and turned around. Admittedly, I had a schedule to follow, but getting to swim in a lake next to a fell on a scorching day like this — you don't get many such chances. So it was a cooling dip. The water was quite clear and good and well bath-warm at the surface. When you stood upright and treaded water, however, it was ice-cold around the feet. (The beach is very small, but there was also a changing room (+), a picnic table (+), a floating dock(+). *Grading:* 3,5/5, mostly thanks to the clear water.)

![Luosto](luosto.jpg)
Aarnilampi, Luosto

From Luosto I continued toward Sodankylä and from there toward Kittilä. Somewhere along this long road stood a van parked just to the right of the road. I'm keeping quite good speed, maybe even slightly over the recommended speed. Just as I approached, two things happened. A police car comes driving in the opposite direction and someone gets out of the parked van, more or less onto the road in front of me. I have to brake hard. Nothing happens, but I suspect the police thought I was braking for them and considered having a chat with me at the very least. But I assume they had more important things to do.

A bit before Kittilä I finally met some non-urban reindeer, who were strolling on the road without any human habitation in sight. I actually recommended to them that they should get some traffic sense, but they just stared stupidly at me.

Just outside Kittilä is a bathing beach by the Ounasjoki. It was packed with bathers. This time I didn't allow myself time to stop and take a dip. I regret it a bit, it would have been cool (pun intended) to swim in the Ounasjoki. In retrospect, it feels a bit like a missed once-in-a-lifetime opportunity. I rode onward to Levi and there I turned up toward the summit. The road up was full of various construction machines and it turned out the last stretch up to the top was completely closed for reconstruction. I had to settle for the view north instead, from a slightly lower point. I snapped some pictures, drank a can of hydration drink I had brought, and then rode down to Sirkka for the next fuel stop. A thermometer on a house wall showed +31 in Levi village - that could be right, it felt like it too.

### Äkäslompolo and Ylläs

Actually, I would have gotten my 1000 miles together without going via Levi and I definitely could have skipped Äkäslompolo. But now I wasn't traveling just to collect miles but also to see the sights. Therefore I had decided to take quite a substantial detour along road 79 toward Muonio and then down to Äkäslompolo. The detour extended the trip by almost 45 minutes but gave me no more miles, since there's no suitable service station where you can fuel to show how you've driven. But as I said, I didn't need the kilometers.

On the road down toward Äkäslompolo I encountered several reindeer. None of them seemed to have more traffic sense than the others. The road was quite bad and quite long. By this time I had already driven a bit over 1000 km and it was certainly starting to feel like I'd been out on the road. Still, it also felt like there's an awfully long way home yet.

### Via Sweden

Then it was just time to burn homeward. Along the quite narrow road out to Kolari there were quite a few cars crawling along, but then out on the main road it flowed well. In Pello I couldn't resist driving over the bridge to Sweden. 100 meters into Sweden I stopped, snapped some photos and then turned around. Now I've been abroad with the bike... Although it would have gone almost as fast to drive down on the Swedish side of the river, but I'll save that for another adventure.

![Sweden](sverige.jpg)

In Ylitornio I refueled and talked with my son for a while. There had been trouble with the headset so I couldn't answer while riding. A reboot seemed to solve the problem.

After driving many hundreds of kilometers through sparsely populated areas, the Tornio valley felt different. I noticed that from Pello down to Tornio there weren't many kilometers between villages and farm groups - completely different from the wastelands in the eastern parts of Northern Ostrobothnia and Lapland.

After Ylitornio I got behind another motorcycle that was keeping quite suitable speed, so I followed all the way down to Tornio. In Tornio he took another road, but after a short bit on the highway I caught up again. He was riding a bit under the speed limit, so I overtook him and noticed his IBA Finland reflective vest. I waved and continued on. Then he overtook me at some point. After a while he slowed down again and I overtook again. And so we continued all the way to Oulu. That was almost the only thing that happened along this boring road. Oh, and the roadwork with 5 minutes of queuing and pilot car in Simo.

### Thunderstorms, night riding and fog

The weather report had promised a day without rain, but if you sit in the saddle for a whole day, you'll get some rain sooner or later. As I drove out of Oulu, a mighty thunderstorm towered up in front of me. The sky was completely black and with one to two seconds between, lightning struck somewhere in the big thunderstorm. It really didn't look appealing to drive into that thunderstorm. I stopped in Limingo to think about what to do.

On my weather app I saw that the thunderstorm was moving from inland and out over the coast. I calculated that I would ride behind the thunderstorm if I waited half an hour and then took the road via Vihanti and Ylivieska. When I set off it wasn't raining, but the clouds were still heavy and it was starting to get properly dark. When I turned off toward Ylivieska I realized my headlight was pointing toward the ground and I could see almost nothing ahead. Moreover, the roads were soaking wet which made it even darker. Salvation came in the form of a Swedish-registered Volvo heading the same way. I hung on behind it, determined not to let go. The thought of standing in pitch darkness and drizzle trying to adjust the headlight didn't appeal. So I hung on, even though it sometimes went a bit too fast for my taste.

When we drove past Vihanti a fire truck with blue lights came driving behind us. We (the Volvo and I) let the fire truck pass. After a while we came to a place with several fire trucks and an ambulance. Probably lightning had struck some house.

I followed the Volvo all the way to Oulainen, but there I had to fuel. All street lights were dark by this time and the clouds were still heavy and dark. So at the ABC service station I fueled just after midnight and then stood there in the humid night adjusting the headlight. But what a difference it made. Now I could see well where I was going. There's a big difference between the old halogen H4 bulb and the new... well, actually... LED bulbs aren't allowed to be mounted in old headlight housings...

At a calm pace I continued through the darkness. Now the temperature started to drop. Not so it got cold, but enough for moisture on the rain-wet ground to rise as fog. South of Ylivieska it got more and more foggy, especially out on open ground. When I got out to the Kokkola road there was locally really thick fog along the ground. Now my illegal headlight didn't help much anymore. Before Kannus the road was newly asphalted and coal black. The only thing to drive by were the posts that mark the edge of the road. In some places there were no posts either. It was so damn dark that I couldn't see anything of the road at all, so I crawled forward at 20-30 km/h. It felt like an eternity before I got to old asphalt again, so I could sense where the road began and ended - and right at the border between old and new asphalt it turned out I was driving on the wrong side of the road, almost in the left ditch. Well, at least the other traffic was almost non-existent. I think I met a couple of trucks and some single car on the whole way from Eskola out to highway 8/E8 in Kokkola.

In Kannus I took a break and put on some more clothes - soon 22 hours in the saddle was taking its toll, now I was probably quite tired and it was starting to feel chilly (though it was certainly still +20). ABC in Karleby was closed, no coffee there. The last stretch home was now more or less torture. I was tired, cold and by this time quite sore in the butt (Saddle Sore!). And to this came the rain-wet roads that absorbed all light. In Nykarleby the rain had apparently stopped, from here the roads were almost dry. And moreover, it was starting to get light a bit.

### Homecoming and summary

I did the final fueling at the same pump I started at and the price per liter was still exactly the same. The time on the receipt was 3:08, so I had spent 22:56 on the road. The official driving distance was 1698 km, while the odometer showed 1821 km. In total, including the way to start and home from finish, I drove 1836 km. I had fueled 80.62 liters of gasoline for 134.54 €. That gives an average consumption of around 4.4 €/100 km. There you can perhaps note that by lowering the speed you can save a bit there. On the stretch Oulainen - Vaasa the average consumption was 3.8 l/100km.

![Map of the route](karta.webp)

Then it was really nice to come home. Usually when I come home I feel I'd gladly have ridden a bit more. That wasn't the case this time.

The following day I was quite tired and despite having drunk quite a lot of water from my backpack I was probably a bit dehydrated too. It took a couple of days before I felt normal again.

As I mentioned, I was probably quite sore in the butt. I had a bit of back pain too, but that went away quickly. The worst was perhaps some kind of soreness in the throttle hand from all the vibrations, which was probably felt for several weeks afterward. And I actually didn't go out with the bike for a whole week after the long trip. Partly I had other things to do, vacation was over and things needed fixing. But partly the desire was probably gone for a while.

### Did I get a diploma then?

So was my drive accepted by the Iron Butt Association? No, unfortunately not, I sent in all the papers but was rejected. The reason was speeding on the stretch between Haapajärvi and Suomussalmi. The controller calculated that my average speed was too high for me to have been able to keep the speed limit. It sure is a bit annoying! I wasn't in a hurry, yet it went too fast. When I arrived in Suomussalmi, I refueled first and then took a break - if I had done in the opposite way around it wouldn't have been a problem. A planning mistake not to consider the average speed, but at the same time also some coincidences - as I said, I barely put my foot down once on the whole way between Haapajärvi and Suomussalmi, it went a bit too smoothly.

Maybe there will be another chance, but for it to succeed quite a few things need to fall into place. It must be possible to be away from the family for two days, the weather must be good, the bike must be in shape and then it's best to do it during the brightest time of the year.
