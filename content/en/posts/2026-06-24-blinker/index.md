# Loose Connection in the Turn Signal

The left rear turn signal has been acting up for a while. It works sometimes, but especially at higher speed, when the wind bends it backwards, it stops blinking. That's a bit unfortunate, because the car behind then has no idea that I'm about to brake and turn. Today it was time to deal with this problem. And as always, even the smallest fix tends to turn into a major project.

<!--more-->

A closer look suggested that it was probably a loose contact. The indicator light sits at the end of a flexible rubber stalk. When you bend the stalk backwards or forwards, the light stops working. I couldn't find any problem in the bulb holder itself, nor any significant corrosion. So the only thing to do was to unscrew the whole stalk and try to examine the insides. But to remove the stalk, I needed to get at the wires to disconnect them. That ought to have been possible under or behind the seat, if the previous mechanic hadn't decided to replace the standard connectors with crimped connectors. I probably could have cut them under the seat, but the wires were too short for me to fit new connectors there. So then the only option was to remove the rear cowl. And to do that I naturally also had to remove the side panels, and especially the luggage rack. So a small(?) repair once again developed into half a project.

![The Rear Cowl](bakkapan.jpg "There hangs the culprit. The luggage rack is unmounted; the rear cowl still has to come off.")

Once I got the indicator off, the problem was fairly obvious. One of the contacts was supposed to be attached to the bulb socket, but it was completely loose. That was most likely what caused the bad connection. The protective rubber on both contacts had worn through. I soldered a new wire onto the socket and taped the other wire, squeezed everything back into the rubber housing and tested the connections with the multimeter - it works again! This still has to be regarded as a temporary repair: sooner or later the solder joint will give way, or the taped wire will wear through completely. So within something like 10,000 to 20,000 km I should probably replace the indicators with new ones.

![Broken Wires](kablar.jpg "Chafed and detached wires")

![Fixed!](reparerat.jpg "The bulb holder repaired. Now it just has to be fitted back onto the bike.")

I took the opportunity to clean the panels and frame in places you normally can't reach. Surprisingly little dirt inside the bodywork, actually, mostly just dust. Then I cleaned and lubricated the chain while waiting for the cowl to dry.

It was almost midnight before everything was screwed back together again. But now the turn signal works as it should for a while longer!
