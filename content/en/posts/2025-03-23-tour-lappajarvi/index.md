# An Icy Adventure

After yesterday's successful premiere, it was time for a slightly longer ride. This Sunday in March the sun was shining and the temperature was already approaching +10°C when I set off just before noon. The major roads were completely dry and I thought that all paved roads would surely be bare by now. Not quite so, as it would turn out. This would become a considerably more icy adventure than I had imagined.

<!--more-->

First I rode to Tervajoki with the plan to once again follow the northern shore of the Kyrö river. Well away from the main road, I quickly noticed that the night's frost hadn't completely let go yet but had left a thin layer of ice on the tarmac. So I had to take the next available bridge across the river and then follow the main road towards Lappo. Admittedly, the trip plan included several roads of comparable size, but I thought that with an air temperature over +10°C, the ice must soon be melted away.

I rode through Lapua center to then take road 6991 towards Kuortane. Already here I made my first small navigation error and missed a turnoff. I tried to follow the phone's navigator app through the transparent cover on the tank bag. Admittedly the phone probably showed the right way, but somewhat small and without voice navigation, I simply forgot to look. No problem, a quick U-turn and the matter was fixed. The road towards Kuortane was then completely snow and ice-free, just hit the gas.

I made my next navigation mistake at the road junction in Vasunmäki. I followed the signs towards Kuortane and turned left. Instead, I had planned to turn right and round Lake Kuortane on the southern side. This time I didn't make a U-turn, I'll have to explore that missed road section another time. I curved through Kuortane center anyway and also drove via the sports institute - impressive facilities for a small town!

Then I headed towards Alajärvi and here the journey began to get exciting. First I turned onto road 711 in the direction of Lappajärvi and immediately after onto the much smaller Haarajärventie (road 17519). The road begins with a fairly steep uphill - which was almost completely ice-covered. Only two deep wheel tracks exposed some wet asphalt. Maybe the road is in shadow, I thought, hoping that the rest of the road would be ice-free. Carefully I made my way up the hill, only to discover that the ice cover continued. Apparently the road had been poorly plowed during the winter so a thick, packed layer of ice had formed. It turned out that this entire road all the way to Saukonkylä had the same road conditions. In more open places, half the road was bare, but in places there were only two narrow wheel tracks through the nearly 10 cm thick, more or less hard ice, seasoned with some half-melted ice slush on top. A sensible person would have turned around at the first hill. I rode 18 km hoping it would soon get better... Fortunately, traffic was very sparse. I drove to the side and let one car pass, and I met two cars, both on sections with enough bare road not to be forced up onto the ice edge. It went slowly, but finally I came out unscathed to bare and almost dry asphalt on the other side.

I continued through Alajärvi and then onwards towards Vimpeli. Now I stuck to slightly larger roads for a while, the road conditions were excellent with dry tarmac and the daytime temperature was now over +10°C. In Vimpeli center, a thermometer showed +14°C, but that was probably somewhat of a over-optimistic gauge. In Vimepli I took a well-deserved coffee break at café Veganni.

![Little Blue enjoying the sun](vimpeli.jpg)

With some coffee in my blood, courage returned and now it was again time for more small roads. First through Rantakylä and then Itäkylä. In Itäkylä on the road Ammesmäentie I came to a downhill that is mostly in the shadow of tall trees - and this hill was again covered with ice. This time there weren't even wheel tracks to the tarmac, so it was just ride and hope for the best. The ice was at least quite soft, so it gave some grip, while being hard enough not to be crushed under the tires. It went fine this time too.

Then out onto the main road again towards Lappajärvi. First I rode out to Kivitippu resort and admired the ice-covered lake. The feeling is a bit the same as in, for example, Vuokatti. The landscape is hilly and mostly forested, quite a few people moving around, and people skiing on the ice. The contrast between motorcycle and ice-covered lake is also quite striking. After a short photo session I continued into Lappajärvi center and past the church.

![Bike and ice-covered lake](lappajarvi.jpg)

Then I got the idea, probably because the conditions had been so good so far, to take a swing along the small roads on Kärnäsaari, directly south of Lappajärvi centre. The road became narrower and narrower and at the same time more damp, as a sign that the ice had melted away not too long ago. And here along the road Kärnänlenkki came an uphill and a steep curve, guarded by tall pines on both sides. Here the road was naturally completely ice-covered, with 10 cm deep wheel tracks that didn't reach the asphalt. The ice was solid but wet, really slippery and at the same time uneven. A meeting would have been impossible and in fact it's not even certain that a possibly oncoming car would manage to stop on this surface. While I stood below the hill hesitating, I also looked at the road ditches - if the bike landed there it would be virtually impossible to haul it back up onto the road without help. So here it was probably just carefully drive with both feet down and hope for the best. The tires rode down in the deep wheel tracks, while the boots scraped on the surrounding ice cover, so I had good power to correct with my legs if needed. Luckily no one came against me and I managed to maintain momentum and keep the bike upright on the approximately 250 meter stretch that was ice-covered. But perhaps not with too large a margin.

Now enough with ice, from now on I thought to avoid really small roads. From Lappajärvi I continued to Kauhava, then Ylihärmä and towards Vörå. Somewhere around here my heated grips shut themselves off and refused to heat anymore. It turned out later that the red light means the battery voltage is too low. It got a bit cold around the fingers on the way home. From Vörå I rode to Vähäkyrö, then along the river's northern shore to Kolkki. By this point the roads were completely dry. Then to ABC for fuel and so home.

Today's ride was 354 km. It took considerably longer than planned due to the challenging conditions in places. I at least made it home before dusk. It got a bit cold at the end, partly due to the trouble with the heated grips but partly because the sun began to disappear behind the trees. It became a bit more adventure than planned and perhaps a bit more risk than one should take when riding a motorcycle. This time I felt grateful to come home unscathed.


## New Municipalities

My long-term goal is to take my motorcycle Little Blue to all of Finland's municipalities. During this journey the following new municipalities were added:
* Kuortane
* Alajärvi
* Vindala
* Lappajärvi

Now Little Blue has rolled through 57 of Finland's 308 municipalities.
