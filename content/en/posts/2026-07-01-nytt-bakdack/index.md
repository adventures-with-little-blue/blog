# New rear tyre and a short test ride

Little Blue's rear tyre was starting to get slightly worn. It was about 1 mm below the legal tread depth limit (the legal minimum tread depth is 1 mm). So it was time for a change. I ordered a new tyre, took the rear wheel off the bike, washed the rim a bit and brought it to the tyre workshop. The hope was that the new tyre would be fitted and ready for collection the very same day. And that I would have time to bolt the wheel back onto the bike and sort out a few other small things in the evening. At the end of the process there was, after all, the prospect of a short test ride in the late evening. Rain was forecast from Thursday evening onward, so if the test ride didn't happen that night it would be postponed by several days.

They promised to call from the tire workshop [Skurs däck & service](https://www.skurs-dack.fi) when the tyre had been changed. I was a little impatient, so I went there at 16:30 after work, but in vain - the tyre had not yet been fitted. Around seven in the evening I got a call from Tobbe saying the tyre was ready to be picked up. The cost was 220 € for a new Michelin Anakee Adventure plus fitting to the rim and balancing. That feels quite expensive if you need to replace the rear tyre every year (and I don't think you'd get it much cheaper anywhere else either).

I cleaned all the parts as well as I could and took the opportunity to check the bearings, rear brakes and so on. Everything seemed to be in order. I also took the opportunity to replace the right-hand chain adjuster. The old one fell off somewhere in Ilomantsi in July 2025. Since then I have been riding with a temporary chain adjuster made from a part of a discarded bicycle saddle, a washer (not stainless) and a lock nut (not stainless). It has actually worked perfectly well. But it does look a bit neater with the proper parts. I ordered the parts from Japan almost a year ago already, but I hadn't needed to touch the chain adjusters until now.

![Chain adjuster](kedjespannare.jpg "Chain adjuster, the old and the new mounted on the piece that the wheel axle passes through. Note that the end plug, washer and two nuts were delivered in four separate bags (OEM).")

After I had adjusted the chain slack and tightened the wheel axle with the torque wrench (90 Nm), I cleaned and lubricated the chain. Almost as good as new. It was now after 22:00, but I still felt that the situation _required_ a little test ride.

![Tyre and chain adjuster installed](nytt_dack.jpg "New tyre and new chain adjuster installed.")

## Test ride

I rode a stretch along the Kyro river from Skatila to Kvevlax. The road is in terrible condition, but the surroundings are beautiful. Since I was passing by anyway, I continued my project of photographing the local church buildings. [Älvkyrkan in Voitby](https://alvbyarna.weebly.com/aumllvkyrkan.html) (sv) used to be called a prayer house, but nowadays it is commonly referred to as a church. After that I also stopped by [Kvevlax church](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kvevlax_kyrka) (sv).

On the way home I still had to stop for fuel. The reserve usually kicks in at around 340 km; now I had already ridden 350 km on one tank, but managed without going onto reserve. The theoretical range (if the tank holds 18 l) is then around 416 km (long-term average), but it varies with the type of road, the load and above all what is called "riding style". Some day I will bring a reserve can with me and see how far I can really get on one tank. At the risk of pulling the dirty sediment from the bottom of the tank into the carburettors.

In any case, the test ride was only 73 km long. It was still after midnight before I got home.

## Result

What a difference! The old tyre, a Metzeler Tourance, was completely worn out and also "square". With the new tyre there was naturally not the same resistance when leaning the bike over. But when I changed the rear tyre last time, from a Metzeler Roadtec to a Tourance, the new tyre did not feel nearly as comfortable on asphalt - clearly worse than the Roadtec. This time, by contrast, I thought the Anakee tyres felt very good on asphalt. They also felt very good on gravel, but here I can't really compare properly - partly because a brand-new 80/20 tyre is of course much better than the worn-out, almost-slick Tourance tyre, and partly because I think I have become a little more confident on gravel since the last change. In any case, the tyres felt stable on gravel. It really makes you wonder what it would feel like to have an Anakee front tyre as well; perhaps it really would make a big difference for gravel riding. Unfortunately there is nothing but road tyres available for my 17-inch front wheel, so I will have to make do with my Roadtec 01 there. Right now, in any case, it feels as though the Anakee was worth the extra few tens it cost compared with a new Tourance.

I have also tried to read up on why my rear tyres wear out so quickly. The Tourance tyre lasted only 12,400 km - and it really was completely worn out. Considering that Little Blue is quite a light bike and that I mostly ride on asphalt, that feels like rather a short lifespan. My theory now is that I have been using too low a tyre pressure. According to Honda's manual, the rear tyre pressure should be 2.25 bar (2.5 bar with a passenger), whereas the tyre manufacturers (Metzeler and Michelin) recommend 2.5 bar normally and 2.8 bar with a passenger. The same goes for the front tyre; Honda recommends 2.0 bar, Metzeler 2.25 bar. So now I am going to raise the pressure and see whether the Anakee lasts 15,000 km.

What was not so good, on the other hand, is that the vibrations I have felt in the handlebars are only getting worse. Partly that may be because higher air pressure makes the tyres harder, but the underlying cause must be something else. One suspicion is that the leaking front forks are causing an imbalance in the damping. Today I clearly felt that the front end was sort of bouncing when the road was uneven. I definitely need to replace the bushes in the front forks as soon as possible. Possibly I ought to replace other things as well, the guide bushes and O-rings, but I can't get hold of those quite immediately. I also need to check the front wheel bearings and the steering head bearings. I am planning a longer trip, but I have to get the vibration problem fixed before that is possible.

As for the chain adjuster, we'll see how long the new one stays there. I think I'll pack my homemade adjuster for the next long trip - just in case.
